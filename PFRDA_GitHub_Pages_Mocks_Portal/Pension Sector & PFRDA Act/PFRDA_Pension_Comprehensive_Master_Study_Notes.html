1. Executive Summary & Demographic Landscape of India

India is experiencing an unprecedented demographic shift. According to Census 2011, the elderly population (age 60+) stood at 10.4 crore (8.6% of population). 2. PFRDA Act, 2013 Statutory Codification & Authority Architecture

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 2013 (Act No. 23 of 2013) received Presidential Assent on September 18, 2013 and was formally notified into force on February 1, 2014 via Notification S.O. 268(E). It extends to the whole of India and replaces the interim executive body created in 2003.

Statutory Chapters of the PFRDA Act, 2013

  • Chapter I (Sec 1–2): Preliminary, Territorial extent (Whole of India), Definitions (CRA, Intermediary, Pension Fund, Subscriber, PRAN, Trustee Bank).
  • Chapter II (Sec 3–11): Establishment of Authority, Head Office at New Delhi (NCR), Meetings (quorum, voting, Chairperson presiding).
  • Chapter III (Sec 12–13): Transfer of assets, liabilities, and employees of Interim PFRDA.
  • Chapter IV (Sec 14–19): Duties, Powers, and Functions of PFRDA; Section 19: Pension Advisory Committee (PAC) — max 25 members representing interests of commerce, industry, agriculture, workers, and subscribers.
  • Chapter V (Sec 20–27): National Pension System & Registration of Intermediaries. Sec 20(2)(g): No government guarantee of returns; Sec 23: Statutory protection of pension wealth against court attachment; Sec 27: Unbundled architecture registration.
  • Chapter VI (Sec 28–35): Penalties and Adjudication. Adjudicating Officer appointed under Sec 31. Sec 34: All penalties credited to the Consolidated Fund of India; Sec 35: Compounding of offences.
  • Chapter VII (Sec 36–38): Appeals to Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) within 45 days (extendable on sufficient cause). Appeal from SAT to Supreme Court within 60 days solely on questions of law.
  • Chapter VIII (Sec 39–46): Finance, Accounts, Audit. Section 40: PFRDA General Fund; Section 43: Allocation to General Reserve; Section 46: CAG Annual Audit laid before Parliament.
  • Chapter IX (Sec 47–54): Miscellaneous. Sec 47: Central Govt binding directions; Sec 48: Supersession of Authority (max 6 months); Sec 49: Good faith indemnity; Sec 50: Cognizance of offences; Sec 51: Overriding effect; Sec 52: Rules by Central Govt; Sec 53: Regulations by PFRDA; Sec 54: 30-day Parliamentary laying rule.
📊 High-Yield Exam Trap: Penalty Ceilings under Chapter VI

Section 28 (Failure to furnish returns/information): ₹1,00,000 per day during default, subject to maximum of ₹1 Crore.
Section 29 (Failure to comply with directions): Min ₹1 Lakh up to ₹1 Crore, plus up to ₹1 Lakh/day for continuing failure.
Section 30 (Failure to resolve subscriber grievances): ₹1,00,000 per day up to maximum of ₹1 Crore.
Destination of Penalties: Always the Consolidated Fund of India (Section 34), NOT the PFRDA General Fund.

3. PFRDA (Exits and Withdrawals under NPS) Regulations & 2025 Amendments

PFRDA notified sweeping structural revisions under the PFRDA (Exits and Withdrawals under the National Pension System) Amendment Regulations, 2025. These changes dramatically enhance subscriber flexibility and liquidity.

Corpus ≤ ₹8 Lakh: 100% Lump Sum Withdrawal • ₹8 Lakh to ₹12 Lakh Slab: Max ₹6L (80%) upfront, balance in SUR (≥ 6 yrs) or Annuity • Corpus > ₹12 Lakh: Up to 80% Lump Sum : Min 20% Annuity (Non-Govt Subscribers; earlier 60:40 ratio) Premature / Voluntary Exit (< Age 60 / <15 yrs) • Corpus ≤ ₹5 Lakh: 100% Lump Sum Withdrawal (Increased from earlier limit of ₹2.5 Lakh) • Corpus > ₹5 Lakh: Min 80% Annuity mandatory : Max 20% Lump Sum • Min subscription tenure: 5 years

✔ Systematic Lump-Sum Withdrawal (SLW) vs Systematic Unit Redemption (SUR)

SLW (Notified late 2023): Allows subscribers at normal exit to withdraw the 60% (or up to 80%) lump-sum portion in staggered monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, or annual installments up to age 75 (now 85).
SUR (2025 Regulations): Mandatory phased payout mechanism of at least 6 years for the remaining balance in the ₹8 Lakh to ₹12 Lakh corpus bracket.

4. Multiple Scheme Framework (MSF) & Investment Architecture

To eliminate rigidity in pension asset allocation, PFRDA introduced the path-breaking Multiple Scheme Framework (MSF) for non-government subscribers.

Core Tenets of Multiple Scheme Framework (MSF)

  • Single PRAN, Multiple Schemes: Non-government subscribers can simultaneously invest in multiple schemes and different Pension Fund Managers (PFMs) under a single Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN).
  • Standardized 5-Category Matrix (Categories A to E): Schemes are categorized based on their equity risk profile:
    • Category A (High Equity Exposure): Up to 100% Equity Exposure permitted. This breaks the historic 75% cap of Active Choice!
    • Category B: Up to 75% Equity Exposure.
    • Category C: Up to 50% Equity Exposure.
    • Category D: Up to 25% Equity Exposure.
    • Category E (Conservative / Pure Debt): 0% Equity (100% fixed income / debt instruments).
  • Risk-Based Classification: Allows aggressive investors to seek pure alpha during accumulation while holding separate conservative schemes for core capital preservation.

6. NPS Vatsalya, APY & Micro-Pension Architecture

NPS Vatsalya (Launched September 18, 2024)

NPS Vatsalya extends the pension accumulation horizon from childhood. It is designed to harness the power of long-term compounding.

  • Eligibility: All minor citizens of India (aged below 18 years).
  • Account Operation: Opened and operated by parent / legal guardian on behalf of the minor. KYC of both guardian and minor required.
  • Minimum Contribution: ₹250 at account opening and a minimum of ₹250 per financial year. (No maximum ceiling).
  • Asset Allocation: Choice between Active Choice (E, C, G up to 75% equity) or Auto Choice Lifecycle Funds.
  • Transition at Majority (Age 18): Upon attaining age 18, the account seamlessly converts into a standard NPS Tier-I PRAN. Fresh KYC of the major subscriber is completed, and they assume full operational control.
  • Tax Deductions: Parents' contribution to NPS Vatsalya shares the exclusive ₹50,000 deduction window under Section 80CCD(1B).

Atal Pension Yojana (APY) Operational Matrix

  • Target Group: All citizens aged 18 to 40 years (primarily informal/unorganized sector). Pension commences at age 60 (minimum 20-year contribution).
  • Guaranteed Pension Slabs: Fixed pension of ₹1,000, ₹2,000, ₹3,000, ₹4,000, or ₹5,000 per month.
  • Return of Corpus (ROC) to Nominee: ₹1.7 Lakh (for ₹1k slab), ₹3.4 Lakh (₹2k), ₹5.1 Lakh (₹3k), ₹6.8 Lakh (₹4k), and ₹8.5 Lakh (for ₹5k slab).
  • Tax Payer Disqualification Rule: Since October 1, 2022, any individual who is or has been an income tax payer under the Income-tax Act, 1961 is strictly ineligible to join APY.
  • Default & Inactivity Penalties:
    • ₹1/month for monthly contribution up to ₹100.
    • ₹2/month for contribution ₹101 to ₹500.
    • ₹5/month for contribution ₹501 to ₹1,000.
    • ₹10/month for contribution > ₹1,000.
    • Account operations frozen after 6 months of non-payment; deactivated after 12 months; closed after 24 months.

8. Annuity Plans, Longevity Economics & Portfolio Mathematics

Taxonomy of Annuity Options

  • Annuity for Life without Return of Purchase Price (ROP): Payout ceases on annuitant's death; principal absorbed into mortality pool. Offers the highest annuity rate.
  • Annuity for Life with Return of Purchase Price (ROP): Lower payout rate; 100% of initial purchase price refunded to nominee upon death.
  • Joint Life Last Survivor with ROP: 100% annuity paid to subscriber for life → 100% annuity continues to surviving spouse for life → 100% purchase price refunded to children/nominees. Mandatory default option for government subscribers under NPS.
  • Annuity Certain: Payout guaranteed for fixed period (5, 10, 15, or 20 years) regardless of whether annuitant survives; if annuitant outlives period, annuity continues for life.

Core Portfolio & Investment Mathematics

Concept Mathematical Formula Interpretation & Application
Yield to Maturity (YTM)
Price = ∑ [C / (1 + YTM)^t] + [Face Value / (1 + YTM)^n]
 Internal rate of return equating discounted bond cash flows to current market price.
Macaulay Duration
D_mac = [∑ t × PV(CF_t)] / Bond Price
 Weighted average maturity of cash flows in years. For a zero-coupon bond, D_mac = Maturity. For a perpetuity: D_mac = (1 + y) / y.
Modified Duration
D_mod = D_mac / (1 + y/k)
 Measures percentage price volatility: % ΔPrice ≈ - D_mod × ΔYield.
Convexity
ΔPrice ≈ [- D_mod × Δy] + [0.5 × Convexity × (Δy)^2]
 Curvature adjustment. Higher positive convexity causes prices to rise more when yields fall and fall less when yields rise.
Portfolio Immunization
Duration of Assets = Duration of Liabilities (D_A = D_L)
 Price risk and reinvestment risk exactly offset each other, locking in target yield regardless of interest rate shifts.
Model / Metric Formula Key Insights
Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)
E(R_i) = R_f + β_i × [E(R_m) - R_f]
 Expected return depends solely on systematic risk (β). Plots the Security Market Line (SML).
Beta (β)
β_i = Cov(R_i, R_m) / Var(R_m)
 Sensitivity to broad market index movements. Cash β = 0.0; Market β = 1.0.
Gordon Growth Model (DDM)
P_0 = D_1 / (r - g) = [D_0 × (1 + g)] / (r - g)
 Values stock based on constant-growth perpetual dividends (requires r > g).
Rule of 72
Doubling Time (Years) ≈ 72 / CAGR (%)
 Mental math rule: at 8% CAGR, money doubles in 72/8 = 9 years; at 9%, in 8 years.
Fisher Effect
(1 + Nominal Rate) = (1 + Real Rate) × (1 + Inflation Rate)
 Approximation: Real Rate ≈ Nominal Rate - Inflation Rate.
Performance Ratio Formula Denominator Used Best Used For
Sharpe Ratio
Sharpe = (R_p - R_f) / σ_p
 Total Risk (σ_p, Standard Deviation) Non-diversified portfolios or standalone funds where total volatility matters.
Treynor Ratio
Treynor = (R_p - R_f) / β_p
 Systematic Risk (β_p, Beta) Well-diversified portfolios (like large pension funds) where unsystematic risk is zero.
Jensen's Alpha (α)
α_p = R_p - [R_f + β_p × (R_m - R_f)]
 Abnormal return (Actual vs CAPM) Quantifies fund manager's value-add / stock-picking alpha. Positive α = outperformance.
Information Ratio
IR = (R_p - R_benchmark) / Tracking Error
 Tracking Error (σ of active returns) Measures consistency of active fund management outperforming a benchmark.
Sortino Ratio
Sortino = (R_p - MAR) / Downside Deviation
 Downside Deviation (semi-variance) Penalizes only harmful downside volatility while ignoring beneficial upside surges.
