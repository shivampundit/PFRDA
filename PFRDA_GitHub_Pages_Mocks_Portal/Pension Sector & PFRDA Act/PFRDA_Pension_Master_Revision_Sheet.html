1. PFRDA Act, 2013 & Statutory Framework Master Guide

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 2013 (Act No. 23 of 2013) was enacted by Parliament to promote old-age income security by establishing an Authority to develop and regulate pension funds and protect the interests of subscribers.

1.1 Statutory Milestones & Commencement

Milestone Date / Provision Significance / Legal Details
Presidential Assent September 18, 2013 Published in Gazette of India Extraordinary Part II Section 1.
Statutory Commencement February 1, 2014 Notified under Section 1(3) via Notification S.O. 268(E) dated January 31, 2014. Replaced Interim PFRDA (set up in 2003).
Territorial Extent Whole of India Section 1(2): Applies to all subscribers and intermediaries across India.

Cooling-Off Period (Sec 13) 2 years from ceasing office: Cannot accept employment in any pension intermediary without Central Govt approval. N/A
Removal Grounds (Sec 12) Insolvent, convicted of moral turpitude, physically/mentally incapable, acquired prejudicial interest, or abused position detrimental to public interest.

3.2 Investment Architecture: Active Choice, Auto Choice & Multiple Scheme Framework (MSF) 2024–2026 Latest

Framework Core Mechanism Asset Allocation & Equity Exposure Caps
Standard Active Choice Subscriber actively decides percentage allocation across E, C, G, A. E (Equity): Max 75% up to age 50 (tapers by 2.5%/yr to 50% by age 60).
C (Corporate Debt): Up to 100%.
G (Government Securities): Up to 100%.
A (Alternative Assets): Max 5%.
Auto Choice (Lifecycle Funds) Automatic reallocation based on age matrix. LC75 (Aggressive): 75% Equity up to age 35 → tapers by 4%/yr to 15% at age 55.
LC50 (Moderate - Default): 50% Equity up to age 35 → tapers by 2%/yr to 10% at age 55.
LC25 (Conservative): 25% Equity up to age 35 → tapers by 1%/yr to 5% at age 55.
Multiple Scheme Framework (MSF) New Allows non-government subscribers to hold multiple schemes under a single PRAN. • High-risk MSF schemes can carry up to 100% equity exposure (exceeding standard 75% Active Choice cap).
• Standardised into 5 Categories (A to E) based on equity exposure:
  – Category A: Highest equity allocation (up to 100%).
  – Category B to D: Intermediate risk profiles.
  – Category E: Lowest equity allocation (conservative/debt-heavy).

3.3 NPS Vatsalya Scheme (Launched 2024 for Minors) Updated

NPS Vatsalya Operational Architecture
  • Eligibility: Minors (below 18 years of age), opened and operated by natural or legal parent/guardian.
  • Contribution Thresholds: Minimum contribution is ₹250 at opening and minimum ₹250 per annum (no upper limit).
  • Seamless Major Transition: Upon the minor attaining 18 years of age, the account seamlessly converts into a regular standard NPS Tier-I All-Citizen PRAN account (fresh KYC of the adult subscriber required).
  • Tax Benefit for Parents: Parent's contribution eligible under Section 80CCD(1B) within the shared cumulative cap of ₹50,000 (Old Tax Regime).
  • Partial Withdrawal: Permitted up to 25% of guardian contributions after 3 years for education, specified illnesses, or disability (max 3 times).

3.4 Partial Withdrawals & Lien/Loan Facility 2024–2025 Rules

Tenure Requirement Minimum 3 years of subscription from date of joining.

6. Other Key Pension & Retirement Schemes in India

6.1 Employees' Pension Scheme, 1995 (EPS-95)

  • Statutory Framework: Administered by EPFO under the EPF & MP Act, 1952.
  • Wage Ceiling: ₹15,000 per month.
  • Contributions: Employer contributes 8.33% (out of total 12% EPF share) up to wage ceiling; Central Govt contributes 1.16%.
  • Pension Formula:
    Monthly Member's Pension = (Pensionable Salary × Pensionable Service) / 70
  • Supreme Court Ruling (Nov 2022): Upheld the 2014 amendment while extending joint option for employees who were members as of Sept 1, 2014, to contribute on actual higher wages.

6.2 Unorganized Worker Schemes: PM-SYM, PM-KMDY, PM-LVM

Age Bracket 18 to 40 years 18 to 40 years 18 to 40 years
Income / Criteria Monthly income ≤ ₹15,000 Landholding ≤ 2 hectares GST Turnover ≤ ₹1.5 Crore
Assured Pension ₹3,000 / month after age 60 ₹3,000 / month after age 60 ₹3,000 / month after age 60
Funding Mechanism 50:50 equal monthly contribution (₹55 to ₹200) by worker and Central Government. 50:50 equal monthly contribution by farmer and Central Government. 50:50 equal monthly contribution by trader and Central Government.

6.3 Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972

  • Eligibility: Minimum 5 years of continuous service (waived in case of death or disablement).
  • Calculation Formula:
    Gratuity = (15 / 26) × Last Drawn Monthly Wage (Basic + DA) × Completed Years of Service
  • Seasonal Establishments: 7 days' wages for each season worked.
  • Maximum Statutory & Tax-Exempt Ceiling: ₹20 Lakh (₹20,00,000) under Section 10(10).

7. Annuity Plans & Longevity Economics Master Summary

7.1 Types of Annuity Contracts

Annuity Variant Payout Mechanism Death Benefit (Nominee) Relative Payout Rate
Life Annuity (without ROP) Guaranteed periodic pension for life of annuitant. Nil. Payouts cease immediately upon death; capital absorbed in mortality pool. Highest Payout Rate (Mortality credits boosted)
Life Annuity with Return of Purchase Price (ROP) Guaranteed periodic pension for life of annuitant. 100% of original purchase price (capital) refunded to nominee. Moderate Payout Rate
Joint Life Last Survivor (without ROP) Pension paid to subscriber for life; on death, 100% continues to spouse for life. Nil on death of both spouses. Moderate-Low Payout Rate
Joint Life Last Survivor with ROP Pension paid to subscriber for life; on death, 100% continues to spouse for life. 100% purchase price refunded to nominee upon demise of surviving spouse. Lowest Payout Rate (Default for Govt employees)
Annuity Certain (5, 10, 15, 20 Years) Guaranteed pension for fixed chosen years whether annuitant lives or dies. If death occurs within period, payments continue to nominee until period ends. Varies by selected term
Increasing / Inflation-Indexed Annuity Payout increases annually at fixed rate (e.g. 3–5%) or tracks CPI. Depends on whether ROP is attached. Lower initial payout; escalates over time

7.2 Longevity Economics Principles

  • Longevity Risk: The financial risk that a retiree outlives their accumulated personal savings, facing poverty in late old age. Annuities transfer this risk to life insurers.
  • Mortality Credits: In life annuities without ROP, the capital of individuals who die earlier than average remains in the risk pool and cross-subsidizes higher payments for long-lived survivors.
  • Adverse Selection: Healthier individuals with higher life expectancy are more likely to buy voluntary annuities, forcing insurers to price for longer lives. Mandatory annuitization in NPS mitigates adverse selection.

8. Basics of Investment & Portfolio Management for Pension Funds

8.1 Core Fixed Income & Bond Formulas

Concept Mathematical Formula Interpretation & Application
Yield to Maturity (YTM)
Price = ∑ [C / (1 + YTM)^t] + [Face Value / (1 + YTM)^n]
 Internal rate of return equating discounted bond cash flows to current market price.
Macaulay Duration
D_mac = [∑ t × PV(CF_t)] / Bond Price
 Weighted average maturity of cash flows in years. For a zero-coupon bond, D_mac = Maturity. For a perpetuity: D_mac = (1 + y) / y.
Modified Duration
D_mod = D_mac / (1 + y/k)
 Measures percentage price volatility: % ΔPrice ≈ - D_mod × ΔYield.
Convexity
ΔPrice ≈ [- D_mod × Δy] + [0.5 × Convexity × (Δy)^2]
 Curvature adjustment. Higher positive convexity causes prices to rise more when yields fall and fall less when yields rise.
Portfolio Immunization
Duration of Assets = Duration of Liabilities (D_A = D_L)
 Price risk and reinvestment risk exactly offset each other, locking in target yield regardless of interest rate shifts.

8.2 Equity Valuation & Risk-Return Metrics

Model / Metric Formula Key Insights
Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)
E(R_i) = R_f + β_i × [E(R_m) - R_f]
 Expected return depends solely on systematic risk (β). Plots the Security Market Line (SML).
Beta (β)
β_i = Cov(R_i, R_m) / Var(R_m)
 Sensitivity to broad market index movements. Cash β = 0.0; Market β = 1.0.
Gordon Growth Model (DDM)
P_0 = D_1 / (r - g) = [D_0 × (1 + g)] / (r - g)
 Values stock based on constant-growth perpetual dividends (requires r > g).
Rule of 72
Doubling Time (Years) ≈ 72 / CAGR (%)
 Mental math rule: at 8% CAGR, money doubles in 72/8 = 9 years; at 9%, in 8 years.
Fisher Effect
(1 + Nominal Rate) = (1 + Real Rate) × (1 + Inflation Rate)
 Approximation: Real Rate ≈ Nominal Rate - Inflation Rate.

8.3 Portfolio Performance Evaluation Ratios

Performance Ratio Formula Denominator Used Best Used For
Sharpe Ratio
Sharpe = (R_p - R_f) / σ_p
 Total Risk (σ_p, Standard Deviation) Non-diversified portfolios or standalone funds where total volatility matters.
Treynor Ratio
Treynor = (R_p - R_f) / β_p
 Systematic Risk (β_p, Beta) Well-diversified portfolios (like large pension funds) where unsystematic risk is zero.
Jensen's Alpha (α)
α_p = R_p - [R_f + β_p × (R_m - R_f)]
 Abnormal return (Actual vs CAPM) Quantifies fund manager's value-add / stock-picking alpha. Positive α = outperformance.
Information Ratio
IR = (R_p - R_benchmark) / Tracking Error
 Tracking Error (σ of active returns) Measures consistency of active fund management outperforming a benchmark.
Sortino Ratio
Sortino = (R_p - MAR) / Downside Deviation
 Downside Deviation (semi-variance) Penalizes only harmful downside volatility while ignoring beneficial upside surges.
