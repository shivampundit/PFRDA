Module 1: Financial System & Regulatory Bodies

Institutional Architecture

1. Structure of the Indian Financial System

The Indian Financial System consists of four primary interdependent pillars: Financial Institutions (Banking & Non-Banking), Financial Markets (Money, Capital, Forex, Debt), Financial Instruments (Short-term, Long-term, Hybrid, Synthetic), and Financial Services (Underwriting, Custodial, Rating, Depository).

Apex Regulators & Statutory Enactments:
  • Reserve Bank of India (RBI): Established on April 1, 1935 under the RBI Act, 1934 based on the Hilton Young Commission. Regulates money market, government securities, forex, commercial banks, cooperative banks, and NBFCs.
  • Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI): Established as an executive body in 1988, given statutory powers on January 30, 1992 through the SEBI Act, 1992. Regulates capital markets, stock exchanges, intermediaries, mutual funds, portfolio managers, and credit rating agencies.
  • Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI): Statutory body established under the IRDA Act, 1999 following the Malhotra Committee recommendations. Headquarters in Hyderabad. Regulates life and general insurance companies, reinsurers, and brokers.
  • Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA): Established by executive resolution in 2003, statutory status granted via the PFRDA Act, 2013 (effective February 1, 2014). Regulates the National Pension System (NPS), Atal Pension Yojana (APY), pension funds, and pension intermediaries.
  • Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI): Established on October 1, 2016 under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC). Regulates insolvency professionals, insolvency professional agencies, and information utilities.
  • Financial Stability and Development Council (FSDC): Non-statutory apex council constituted in December 2010 on the recommendations of the Raghuram Rajan Committee. Chaired by the Union Finance Minister. Members include heads of RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, IBBI, Finance Secretary, Chief Economic Adviser, and IT Secretary. Sub-committee of FSDC is chaired by the Governor of RBI.

2. Prudential Framework & Regulatory Norms

Regulatory Norm Prescribed Metric / Ratio Statutory / Regulatory Purpose
CRAR (Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio) 9.0% for Commercial Banks (11.5% including Capital Conservation Buffer CCB of 2.5%); 12.0% for Public Sector Banks; 15.0% for Small Finance Banks. Basel III capital adequacy to absorb unexpected loan losses without insolvency.
Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) 100% of Net Cash Outflows over a 30-day stress horizon. Ensure banks hold sufficient High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to survive acute short-term stress.
Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) Minimum 100% (Available Stable Funding / Required Stable Funding). Promote resilience over a 1-year horizon by requiring banks to fund activities with stable sources.
Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework Triggers based on: (1) Capital (CRAR/CET-1), (2) Asset Quality (Net NPA ratio ≥ 6%), (3) Leverage Ratio. Early supervisory intervention by RBI before a bank's financial condition deteriorates irreversibly.
Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) for NBFCs Four-layered structure: Base Layer (NBFC-BL < ₹1000 cr), Middle Layer (NBFC-ML), Upper Layer (NBFC-UL - top 25-30), Top Layer (NBFC-TL - currently empty). Proportionality in regulation: systemically important NBFCs face bank-like capital and governance norms.

Module 2: Financial Markets (Primary & Secondary)

Money, Bond, Equity & Forex

1. Money Market Instruments & Architecture

The money market is the market for short-term financial assets with maturities up to one year. It provides liquidity management mechanisms for banks, financial institutions, corporates, and the Government.

Instrument Issuer & Investor Eligibility Tenor / Maturity Minimum Denomination & Key Rules
Treasury Bills (T-Bills) Issued by Government of India via RBI; Zero-coupon discounted instruments. 91-day, 182-day, and 364-day tenors. (14-day intermediate bills for states). Minimum ₹10,000 and multiples thereof. Issued at discount, redeemed at par. State governments cannot issue T-Bills.
Cash Management Bills (CMBs) Issued by GoI via RBI to meet temporary cash flow mismatches. Maturity strictly less than 91 days. Issued at discount to face value; minimum ₹10,000.
Commercial Paper (CP) Corporates with tangible net worth ≥ ₹4 cr, working capital limit, and minimum credit rating of A3. Minimum 7 days, Maximum up to 1 year. Minimum ₹5 Lakh and in multiples of ₹5 Lakh thereof. Unsecured promissory note issued at discount.
Certificate of Deposit (CD) Scheduled Commercial Banks and select All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs). Banks: 7 days to 1 year; AIFIs: 1 year to 3 years. Minimum ₹1 Lakh and multiples of ₹1 Lakh thereof. Dematerialised form.
Call / Notice / Term Money Inter-bank market (Commercial Banks, Cooperative Banks, Primary Dealers). Call: 1 day (overnight); Notice: 2 to 14 days; Term: 15 days to 1 year. Purely unsecured; benchmarked to MIBOR (Mumbai Interbank Offered Rate).
TREPS (Triparty Repo) CCIL (Clearing Corporation of India) acts as central counterparty. 1 day to 1 year (predominantly overnight/short-term). Collateralised borrowing against G-Secs with anonymity and zero counterparty credit risk.

2. Bond Market & Yield Curve Dynamics

The bond market comprises Government Securities (G-Secs), State Development Loans (SDLs), and Corporate Debt Securities.

Key Bond Valuation & Risk Metrics:
• Current Yield: (Annual Coupon Payment / Current Market Price)
• Yield to Maturity (YTM): The internal rate of return (IRR) equating bond's purchase price to present value of future cash flows.
• Macaulay Duration: Weighted average time until cash flows are received: D_mac = ∑ [t × PV(CF_t)] / Price
• Modified Duration (MD): Price sensitivity to interest rate changes: MD = D_mac / (1 + YTM/m) → ΔP / P ≈ -MD × Δy
• Convexity: Second derivative measuring curvature: ΔP / P ≈ -MD × Δy + 0.5 × Convexity × (Δy)^2. Greater convexity protects against rate hikes and enhances gains during rate cuts.

3. Equity Capital Market: Primary & Secondary

  • Book Building Allocation Norms (SEBI ICDR Regulations):
    • Profit-Making Companies (Eligibility under Reg 6(1)): Minimum 35% for Retail Individual Investors (RIIs ≤ ₹2 Lakh application), Minimum 15% for Non-Institutional Investors (NIIs / HNIs), Maximum 50% for Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIBs).
    • Unprofitable / Tech Companies (Eligibility under Reg 6(2)): Minimum 75% for QIBs, Maximum 15% for NIIs, Maximum 10% for Retail.
    • Anchor Investors: Allocated out of the QIB quota up to 60%. Lock-in period: 30 days for 50% of shares, 90 days for remaining 50%.
  • Market Microstructure: Indian equity secondary market operates on a T+1 rolling settlement cycle across all equities (with optional T+0 settlement introduced for top liquid scrips). ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount) is mandatory for IPO applications.

4. Foreign Exchange Market Architecture

Governed by FEMA, 1999 and operational regulations issued by RBI and FEDAI (Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association of India).

  • Direct vs Indirect Quote: Direct Quote expresses 1 unit of foreign currency in domestic currency (e.g. 1 USD = ₹84.00). Indirect Quote expresses 1 unit of domestic currency in foreign currency. In India, foreign exchange is quoted directly.
  • NEER & REER: Nominal Effective Exchange Rate is a trade-weighted basket index. Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) adjusts NEER for relative inflation differentials (using CPI). REER > 100 indicates overvaluation (loss of export competitiveness).
  • Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS): Resident individuals can remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year for permissible current and capital account transactions. TCS (Tax Collected at Source) applies at 20% on remittances above ₹7 Lakh (except for medical/education purposes).

Risk Management Instruments

2. Option Mechanics, Pricing & Greeks

  • Moneyness:
    • Call Option: In-The-Money (ITM) when Spot (S) > Strike (K); At-The-Money (ATM) when S = K; Out-of-The-Money (OTM) when S < K.
    • Put Option: ITM when S < K; ATM when S = K; OTM when S > K.
    • Intrinsic Value: Max(0, S − K) for Call; Max(0, K − S) for Put. Total Option Premium = Intrinsic Value + Time Value.
  • Put-Call Parity: Fundamental arbitrage relationship for European options: C + PV(K) = P + S ⇒ C + K × e^(-rT) = P + S
  • Option Greeks:
    • Delta (Δ): Rate of change of option price w.r.t. underlying price (∂V/∂S). Call Delta ranges from 0 to +1; Put Delta ranges from −1 to 0. ATM option Delta ≈ 0.5.
    • Gamma (Γ): Sensitivity of Delta to changes in underlying price (∂Δ/∂S). Highest for ATM options nearing expiry.
    • Theta (Θ): Time decay of option value (∂V/∂t). Negative for long option holders. Accelerates sharply in the final 30 days before expiration.
    • Vega (ν): Sensitivity of option price to changes in implied volatility (∂V/∂σ). Highest for ATM options.
    • Rho (ρ): Sensitivity of option price to risk-free interest rates (∂V/∂r). Positive for calls, negative for puts.
  • Option Trading Strategies:
    • Straddle: Long Call + Long Put with identical strike and expiry. Profitable during high volatility break-outs in either direction.
    • Strangle: Long OTM Call + Long OTM Put. Cheaper than straddle; requires larger price move.
    • Bull Call Spread: Buy lower strike Call + Sell higher strike Call. Caps upside profit while reducing net premium cost.
    • Protective Put: Long Stock + Long Put. Synthetic Call profile offering downside protection against crash.

Module 4: Financial Inclusion & Priority Sector Lending (PSL)

Financial Deepening & Inclusion

1. Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Mandates

Category of Bank Overall PSL Target Key Mandatory Sub-Targets
Domestic Commercial Banks & Foreign Banks (≥ 20 branches) 40% of ANBC (Adjusted Net Bank Credit) or CEOBE (Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposure), whichever is higher. • Agriculture: 18% of ANBC (within this, 10% for Small & Marginal Farmers - SMFs).
• Micro Enterprises: 7.5% of ANBC.
• Weaker Sections: 12% of ANBC.
Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) & Small Finance Banks (SFBs) 75% of ANBC Focus on microfinance, small/marginal farmers, micro-enterprises, and weaker sections.
Primary Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs) Phased glide path reaching 75% of ANBC by March 31, 2026. Sub-targets for micro-enterprises and weaker sections.

Note on Shortfalls: Banks failing to achieve PSL targets must contribute the shortfall amount to RIDF (Rural Infrastructure Development Fund) managed by NABARD or other funds with SIDBI, MHA, or NHB, earning below-market interest rates.

2. Social Security & Financial Inclusion Flagships

  • PMJDY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana): Universal banking access. RuPay debit card with ₹2 Lakh accidental insurance cover; Overdraft facility up to ₹10,000 (no conditions up to ₹2,000; age 18–65).
  • PMJJBY (Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana): Renewable life insurance of ₹2 Lakh for death due to any cause. Age group: 18–50 years. Annual premium: ₹436 per annum.
  • PMSBY (Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana): Accidental death / permanent total disability cover of ₹2 Lakh (₹1 Lakh for partial disability). Age: 18–70 years. Annual premium: ₹20 per annum.
  • APY (Atal Pension Yojana): Guaranteed minimum pension of ₹1,000, ₹2,000, ₹3,000, ₹4,000, or ₹5,000 per month starting at age 60. Entry age: 18–40 years. Taxpayers barred from enrolling since October 1, 2022. Administered by PFRDA.
  • MUDRA (Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana - PMMY): Refinance agency through commercial banks, RRBs, SFBs, and MFIs:
    • Shishu: Loans up to ₹50,000.
    • Kishore: Loans from ₹50,001 to ₹5 Lakh.
    • Tarun: Loans from ₹5,00,001 to ₹10 Lakh.
    • Tarun Plus (Union Budget 2024-25 enhancement): Loans from ₹10 Lakh up to ₹20 Lakh for entrepreneurs who have successfully repaid past Tarun loans.
  • RBI Financial Inclusion Index (FI-Index): Annual comprehensive measure (0 to 100). No base year. Comprises 3 dimensions: Access (35% weight), Usage (45% weight), and Quality (20% weight).

Module 5: Alternate Sources of Finance & PPP Models

Non-Bank Financing & Infrastructure

1. Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) & Private Equity

Governed by SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012:

  • Category I AIF: Funds that invest in start-ups, early-stage ventures, social ventures, SMEs, or infrastructure with positive economic externalities. Examples: Venture Capital Funds (VCF), Angel Funds, SME Funds, Social Venture Funds, Infrastructure Funds. Minimum investment by an investor: ₹1 Crore (₹25 Lakh for Angel Fund investors).
  • Category II AIF: Funds that do not undertake leverage other than for day-to-day operational requirements. Examples: Private Equity (PE) funds, Debt funds, Fund of Funds.
  • Category III AIF: Funds that employ diverse or complex trading strategies and may employ leverage, including investments in listed or unlisted derivatives. Examples: Hedge Funds, PIPE funds.

2. REITs, InvITs & Social Stock Exchange

  • Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) & Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs): Quasi-debt/equity yield instruments. Must distribute at least 90% of Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF) to unit holders semi-annually. Minimum 80% of assets must be in completed, revenue-generating projects.
  • Social Stock Exchange (SSE): A separate segment within registered stock exchanges (BSE/NSE) enabling Non-Profit Organisations (NPOs) and For-Profit Social Enterprises (FPSEs) to raise capital. NPOs can issue Zero Coupon Zero Principal (ZCZP) instruments. Minimum issue size is ₹50 Lakh and minimum application size is ₹10,000.

3. Public-Private Partnership (PPP) Models

Model Revenue & Financing Risk Asset Ownership & Key Characteristic
BOT (Build-Operate-Transfer) - Toll Private concessionaire bears 100% traffic, financing, and revenue risk. Private party builds and tolls the asset, then transfers back to Govt after concession period (e.g. 20-30 years).
BOT (Annuity) Government bears traffic/revenue risk; Concessionaire bears construction risk. Government pays fixed semi-annual annuity payments to the concessionaire over the concession period.
HAM (Hybrid Annuity Model) Shared risk (40% Government capital support, 60% Private developer debt/equity). NHAI/Govt pays 40% of Project Cost during construction in 5 milestone-linked installments (8% each). The balance 60% is recovered as bi-annual annuity payments linked to inflation and interest rate (repo rate linked).
TOT (Toll-Operate-Transfer) Private investor bears toll collection and maintenance risk; zero construction risk. Operational, revenue-generating government assets are monetised for a 15-30 year period against an upfront lump-sum payment.
VGF (Viability Gap Funding) Government capital subsidy for economically justified but financially unviable projects. Up to 20% of Total Project Cost (TPC) provided by Central Government; sponsoring Ministry/State can provide an additional 20% (Total up to 40% grant).

Module 6: Taxes, GST, Finance Commission, Fiscal Policy & FRBM

Public Finance & Fiscal Architecture

1. Direct & Indirect Taxation Framework

  • New Income Tax Regime (Section 115BAC, updated in Union Budget 2024-25):
    • Nil up to ₹3,00,000
    • ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000: 5%
    • ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 10%
    • ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 15%
    • ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000: 20%
    • Above ₹15,00,000: 30%
    • Standard Deduction increased to ₹75,000 for salaried employees. Tax rebate under Section 87A ensures zero tax liability up to ₹7,00,000 taxable income.
  • Corporate Income Tax: Section 115BAA grants a concessional tax rate of 22% (effective tax rate 25.17% including surcharge and cess) for existing domestic companies that forego deductions. Section 115BAB provides a 15% rate for new manufacturing entities incorporated before March 31, 2024. Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) rate is 15%.
  • Goods and Services Tax (GST): Dual GST model (CGST + SGST / UTGST for intra-state; IGST for inter-state transactions). GST Council constituted under Article 279A of the Constitution: Chaired by the Union Finance Minister; voting power: Centre has one-third (33.33%) weightage and States collectively have two-thirds (66.67%); decision requires a three-fourths (75%) majority.

2. Finance Commission & Fiscal Devolution

Constitutional body constituted every five years under Article 280 by the President of India.

  • 15th Finance Commission (Chaired by N.K. Singh, 2021–2026):
    • Recommended 41% vertical devolution of the divisible tax pool to States (reduced by 1% from 42% of 14th FC to adjust for J&K and Ladakh UTs).
    • Horizontal Devolution Criteria & Weights: Income Distance: 45.0% | Population (2011 Census): 15.0% | Area: 15.0% | Forest & Ecology: 10.0% | Demographic Performance (TFR control): 12.5% | Tax & Fiscal Effort: 2.5%.
  • 16th Finance Commission: Constituted in December 2023 under the Chairmanship of Dr. Arvind Panagariya; report covering 5-year award period 2026–2031 due by October 31, 2025.

3. Fiscal Deficit Concepts & FRBM Act, 2003

Key Deficit Formulas:
• Fiscal Deficit (FD): Total Expenditure − (Revenue Receipts + Non-Debt Capital Receipts) = Borrowings & other liabilities.
• Revenue Deficit (RD): Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts.
• Effective Revenue Deficit (ERD): Revenue Deficit − Grants for Creation of Capital Assets.
• Primary Deficit (PD): Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments. Measures the current year's fiscal stance excluding past legacy debt obligations.
  • FRBM Act, 2003 & N.K. Singh Review Committee:
    • Recommended targeting Debt-to-GDP ratio of 60% (40% for Central Government, 20% for State Governments).
    • Fiscal deficit target of 3.0% of GDP.
    • Escape Clause: Allows up to 0.5% of GDP deviation from fiscal targets under conditions of national security, war, national calamity, collapse of agriculture, or structural reforms with unanticipated fiscal implications.

2. Specialized CPI Series & Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT)

  • Labour Bureau CPI Series (Ministry of Labour & Employment):
    • CPI-IW (Industrial Workers): Base Year 2016 (revised from 2001). Used for calculating Dearness Allowance (DA) for central government employees and industrial workers.
    • CPI-AL (Agricultural Labourers) & CPI-RL (Rural Labourers): Base Year 1986–87. Used for revising minimum wages and MGNREGA wage rates.
  • Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) Framework:
    • Adopted under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, 1934 (amended via Finance Act, 2016).
    • Target: 4.0% headline CPI inflation with a tolerance band of +/- 2% (Floor: 2.0%, Ceiling: 6.0%).
    • Monetary Policy Committee (MPC): 6 members (3 from RBI including Governor who has casting vote, and 3 external members appointed by Central Government for a 4-year term, non-reappointable).
    • Failure of Monetary Policy: If average headline CPI inflation remains outside the 2%–6% band for three consecutive quarters, RBI must submit a statutory explanatory report to the Central Government detailing: (1) reasons for failure, (2) remedial actions, and (3) estimated time-frame to return to the target.
