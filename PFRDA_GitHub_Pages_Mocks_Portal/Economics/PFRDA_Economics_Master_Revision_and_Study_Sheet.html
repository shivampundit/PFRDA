1. Demand and Supply, Elasticity & Consumer Behaviour

A. Law of Demand and Movement vs. Shift

  • Law of Demand: Ceteris paribus (other things equal), there is an inverse relationship between own price and quantity demanded: $P \uparrow \rightarrow Q_d \downarrow$.
  • Movement along the Curve: Caused strictly by a change in the own price of the good (expansion/contraction of demand).
  • Shift of the Curve: Caused by changes in non-price determinants (consumer income, tastes/preferences, prices of substitutes/complements, demographic changes, future price expectations).
  • Exceptions to the Law of Demand:
    • Giffen Goods: Extreme inferior staple goods where negative income effect outweighs the substitution effect, resulting in an upward-sloping demand curve ($P \uparrow \rightarrow Q_d \uparrow$).
    • Veblen Goods (Conspicuous Consumption): Luxury prestige items (Rolex, sports cars) where high price signals social status ($P \uparrow \rightarrow Q_d \uparrow$).
    • Speculative Goods: Buyers expect prices to rise further, purchasing more at higher prices.
Price Elasticity of Demand (Point Method): $e_p = \frac{\% \Delta Q_d}{\% \Delta P} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$
Geometric Formula on Linear Curve: $e_p = \frac{\text{Lower Segment}}{\text{Upper Segment}}$
• Midpoint: $e_p = 1$ (Unitary)
• Upper segment: $e_p > 1$ (Elastic)
• Lower segment: $e_p < 1$ (Inelastic)
• Vertical intercept: $e_p = \infty$ (Perfect Elasticity)
• Horizontal intercept: $e_p = 0$ (Perfect Inelasticity)
Cross-Price Elasticity ($e_{xy}$): $e_{xy} > 0$ for Substitutes (tea/coffee); $e_{xy} < 0$ for Complements (car/petrol); $e_{xy} = 0$ for Independent goods.
Income Elasticity ($e_y$): $e_y > 0$ for Normal goods ($e_y > 1$ for Luxury; $0 < e_y < 1$ for Necessity); $e_y < 0$ for Inferior goods.

B. Ordinal Utility & Consumer Equilibrium

  • Indifference Curve (IC): Locus of points representing combinations of two goods providing identical satisfaction/utility to the consumer.
  • Properties of ICs: Negative slope (downward), Convex to the origin (due to Diminishing Marginal Rate of Substitution — $MRS_{xy} = MU_x / MU_y$), Higher IC represents higher utility, ICs cannot intersect.
  • Consumer Equilibrium: Occurs where the Budget Line is tangent to the highest attainable Indifference Curve: $MRS_{xy} = \frac{P_x}{P_y} = \frac{MU_x}{MU_y}$.
  • Price Effect Decomposition: $\text{Total Price Effect} = \text{Substitution Effect} + \text{Income Effect}$.
    • Hicksian Decomposition: Compensates income to keep consumer on the original Indifference Curve (constant utility).
    • Slutsky Decomposition: Compensates income so the consumer can just afford the original consumption basket at the new relative price ratio (constant purchasing power).

2. Theory of Production, Costs & Market Structures

A. Production & Cost Functions

  • Law of Variable Proportions (Short Run): Adding variable input to fixed factors yields: Stage I (Increasing Marginal Returns), Stage II (Diminishing Positive Returns — rational production stage), Stage III (Negative Marginal Returns).
  • Cobb-Douglas Production Function: $Q = A \cdot L^\alpha \cdot K^\beta$.
    • $\alpha + \beta = 1$: Constant Returns to Scale (CRS).
    • $\alpha + \beta > 1$: Increasing Returns to Scale (IRS).
    • $\alpha + \beta < 1$: Decreasing Returns to Scale (DRS).
    • Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution: $MRTS_{LK} = MP_L / MP_K = w / r$ at producer cost-minimization equilibrium.
  • Cost Curves: Marginal Cost cuts Average Total Cost (ATC) and Average Variable Cost (AVC) from below at their absolute minimum points. Long-Run Average Cost (LAC) is the 'Envelope Curve' enclosing Short-Run Average Cost (SAC) curves.

C. Oligopoly Models & Game Theory

  • Cournot Duopoly: Simultaneous quantity competition. Each firm assumes the rival's output is fixed. Total output = $2/3$ of competitive output; Price is between monopoly and competition.
  • Bertrand Duopoly: Simultaneous price competition with homogeneous goods. Undercutting drives $P = MC$, yielding competitive outcome with just two firms (Bertrand Paradox).
  • Stackelberg Duopoly: Sequential quantity competition. Leader moves first, anticipates follower's response, gaining first-mover advantage and higher profit.
  • Sweezy's Kinked Demand Curve: Explains price stickiness in non-collusive oligopoly. Assumes rivals match price cuts but ignore price hikes, creating a kink in the demand curve and a vertical gap in the Marginal Revenue curve.
  • Game Theory (Nash Equilibrium): A strategy profile where no player has an incentive to unilaterally deviate given others' strategies. In Prisoner's Dilemma, defecting is a dominant strategy, producing a Pareto-suboptimal Nash Equilibrium.

3. National Income Accounting & Circular Flow

Core National Income Identities:
• $\text{Gross National Product (GNP)} = \text{GDP} + \text{Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)}$
• $\text{Net Domestic Product (NDP)} = \text{GDP} - \text{Depreciation (Consumption of Fixed Capital)}$
• $\text{Net National Product at Factor Cost } (NNP_{FC} = \text{National Income}) = NNP_{MP} - \text{Net Indirect Taxes (NIT)}$
• $\text{Net Indirect Taxes (NIT)} = \text{Indirect Taxes} - \text{Subsidies}$
• $\text{GVA at Basic Prices} = \text{GVA at Factor Cost} + (\text{Production Taxes} - \text{Production Subsidies})$
• $\text{GDP at Market Prices} = \text{GVA at Basic Prices} + (\text{Product Taxes} - \text{Product Subsidies})$
• $\text{GDP Deflator} = \left(\frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}}\right) \times 100$

Three Measurement Approaches to GDP

  • Production / Value Added Method: $\text{GVA} = \text{Value of Output} - \text{Intermediate Consumption}$. Avoids double counting.
  • Income Method: $\text{NDP}_{FC} = \text{Compensation of Employees} + \text{Operating Surplus (Rent, Interest, Profit)} + \text{Mixed Income of Self-Employed}$.
  • Expenditure Method: $Y = C + I + G + (X - M)$.
    • $C$ = Private Final Consumption Expenditure (PFCE).
    • $I$ = Gross Capital Formation (Gross Fixed Capital Formation + Change in Stocks).
    • $G$ = Government Final Consumption Expenditure (GFCE).
    • $(X - M)$ = Net Exports.
  • Circular Flow Leakages vs. Injections: In equilibrium, Total Leakages = Total Injections: $S + T + M = I + G + X$.

4. Classical vs. Keynesian Approaches

Dimension Classical Economic School Keynesian Macroeconomic School
Say's Law Accepted: "Supply creates its own demand". General overproduction is impossible. Rejected: Demand creates its own supply. Effective demand determines output.
Wage-Price Behavior Perfectly flexible wages and prices clear markets instantaneously. Sticky wages and prices downwards due to labor contracts and institutional rigidities.
Employment Equilibrium Economy naturally gravitates to full employment. Any unemployment is voluntary. Underemployment equilibrium is normal. Prolonged involuntary unemployment occurs.
Money & Dichotomy Classical Dichotomy holds: Money is neutral, affecting only nominal price levels ($MV=PT$). Money affects real output via interest rate channel. Liquidity preference matters.
Savings-Investment Equality Equilibrated by flexible real interest rate in loanable funds market. Equilibrated by changes in the level of aggregate national income ($Y$).
Role of Government Laissez-faire; balanced budgets; government intervention causes distortions. Active counter-cyclical fiscal intervention required to offset private demand shortfalls.

5. Consumption & Investment Functions

A. Theories of the Consumption Function

  • Keynesian Absolute Income Hypothesis: $C = \bar{C} + cY$. As income rises, $APC$ falls while $0 < MPC < 1$. Explains short-run budget data, but fails Simon Kuznets' long-run constant $APC$ puzzle.
  • Duesenberry's Relative Income Hypothesis (1949): Consumption depends on relative income compared to peers (Demonstration Effect) and past peak income (Ratchet Effect: consumption resists falling when income drops).
  • Friedman's Permanent Income Hypothesis (1957): Income has permanent ($Y_p$) and transitory ($Y_t$) parts. Consumption is proportional to permanent income: $C = k \cdot Y_p$. $MPC$ out of transitory windfalls is close to zero (saved).
  • Modigliani's Life Cycle Hypothesis (LCH): Consumers smooth consumption over their entire lifetime: borrow in youth, accumulate wealth during peak working years, and dissave during retirement. Aggregate national saving depends on demographic age structure.

B. Theories of Investment

  • Marginal Efficiency of Capital (MEC): Internal rate of return equating expected prospective yields of a capital asset over its lifetime to its supply replacement price. Invest if $\text{MEC} > r$.
  • Marginal Efficiency of Investment (MEI): Accounts for rising capital goods prices as economy-wide investment surges; MEI is steeper than MEC.
  • Tobin's q Theory (1969): $q = \frac{\text{Market Value of Capital}}{\text{Replacement Cost of Capital}}$. If $q > 1$, firms issue equity to buy cheap physical machinery, stimulating real investment.

6. Multipliers, Accelerator & Super-Multiplier

Keynesian Investment Multiplier: $k = \frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta I} = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS}$
Tax Multiplier: $k_t = \frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta T} = \frac{-MPC}{1 - MPC} = \frac{-MPC}{MPS}$
Government Spending Multiplier: $k_g = \frac{1}{1 - MPC}$
Balanced Budget Multiplier (Haavelmo Theorem): $k_{bb} = k_g + k_t = \frac{1}{1 - c} - \frac{c}{1 - c} = 1$ (Income expands by exact amount of expenditure: $\Delta Y = \Delta G$)
Foreign Trade Multiplier (Open Economy): $k_f = \frac{1}{MPS + MPM} = \frac{1}{1 - c + m}$
Accelerator Principle (J.M. Clark): $I_t = v(Y_t - Y_{t-1})$ (Investment depends on the rate of change of output)
Super-Multiplier (Samuelson & Hicks): Synergy of multiplier and accelerator driving cumulative business cycles.

Paradox of Thrift (Keynes): An autonomous attempt by all households to increase saving during a slump reduces aggregate consumption, collapsing national income and leaving total aggregate saving unchanged or lower.

7. The IS-LM Framework & Macroeconomic Policy

IS Curve (Goods Market): $Y = C(Y - T) + I(r) + G$. Downward-sloping. Shifts right with expansionary fiscal policy ($\Delta G > 0, \Delta T < 0$).

LM Curve (Money Market): $M/P = L(Y, r)$. Upward-sloping. Shifts right with expansionary monetary policy ($\Delta M > 0$) or falling price level ($\Delta P < 0$).

Special Case IS / LM Shape Monetary Policy Impact Fiscal Policy Impact & Crowding Out
Normal Range IS downward; LM upward Effective: $r \downarrow, Y \uparrow$ Effective: $Y \uparrow, r \uparrow$; Partial crowding-out of private investment.
Liquidity Trap LM perfectly horizontal ($r \approx 0$) 0% Effective: Cash hoarding ('pushing on a string') 100% Maximum Effective: Full multiplier expansion, zero crowding out.
Classical Range LM perfectly vertical ($h = 0$) 100% Maximum Effective: Direct impact on $Y$ 0% Effective: 100% complete crowding-out of private investment.
Investment Inelastic IS perfectly vertical ($b = 0$) 0% Effective: Rate falls, but investment doesn't move 100% Maximum Effective: Full Keynesian expansion.

8. Inflation Dynamics & The Phillips Curve

A. Inflation Categories & Metrics

  • Speed of Inflation: Creeping (<3% p.a.), Walking (3-10%), Running (10-20%), Galloping / Hyperinflation (>50% per month — Phillip Cagan).
  • Causes: Demand-Pull ($AD > AS$), Cost-Push (supply shocks, oil spikes, wage spirals), Structural (developing country supply bottlenecks).
  • Costs of Inflation: Inflation tax (seigniorage), Bracket creep (fiscal drag), Shoe-leather costs, Menu costs, Arbitrary wealth redistribution from Creditors to Debtors.
  • Headline vs. Core Inflation: Headline covers full CPI; Core strips out volatile food and fuel to measure underlying price trends.

B. Phillips Curve Evolution

  • Original Phillips Curve (1958): Empirical inverse trade-off between nominal wage inflation and unemployment. Adapted by Samuelson & Solow to Price Inflation vs Unemployment.
  • Stagflation (1970s): Supply shocks caused simultaneous high inflation and high unemployment, breaking the traditional short-run trade-off.
  • Expectations-Augmented Phillips Curve (Friedman & Phelps): $\pi = \pi^e - \beta(u - u^*) + v$.
    • Short run: Downward-sloping based on expected inflation $\pi^e$.
    • Long run: Perfectly vertical at the Natural Rate of Unemployment / NAIRU (Non-Accelerating Inflation Rate of Unemployment). There is zero long-run trade-off.
  • Rational Expectations (Lucas & Sargent): Policy Ineffectiveness Proposition — fully anticipated monetary policy changes do not fool rational agents, making the Phillips Curve vertical even in the short run.

9. Business Cycles & Economic Fluctuations

  • Four Phases: Expansion (Boom), Peak, Contraction (Recession: ≥ 2 consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth), Trough (Recovery).
  • Major Theories:
    • Schumpeter's Innovation Theory: Waves of entrepreneurial innovation drive cycles via Creative Destruction.
    • Keynesian Multiplier-Accelerator Interaction: Volatile investment ('animal spirits') amplified by super-multiplier swings.
    • Monetarist Theory (Friedman): Unsteady, erratic central bank money supply growth causes booms and busts.
    • Real Business Cycle (RBC) Theory (Kydland & Prescott): Cycles are optimal market equilibrium responses to real technology and productivity supply shocks.
    • Austrian Theory (Hayek & Mises): Artificial central bank interest rate suppression generates credit-fueled malinvestment that eventually crashes.
  • Leading Indicators: Inverted yield curve (10-yr vs 2-yr spread turns negative — powerful recession signal), stock indices, building permits, PMI.

10. Balance of Payments (BOP) Architecture

BOP Accounting Identity: $\text{Current Account} + \text{Capital Account} + \text{Financial Account} + \text{Errors \& Omissions} = 0$
Current Account: Merchandise Balance (Goods) + Services (Software, Travel) + Primary Income (Dividends, Interest) + Secondary Income (Remittances, Grants)
Financial Account: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) + Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) + External Commercial Borrowings (ECB) + NRI Deposits + Reserve Assets
Concept Analytical Definition & Significance
Autonomous Transactions 'Above-the-line' transactions undertaken for independent commercial/profit motives regardless of the BOP balance.
Accommodating Transactions 'Below-the-line' financing transactions undertaken by central banks (drawing down forex reserves, IMF loans) to bridge the autonomous BOP gap.
Current Account Deficit (CAD) Occurs when total imports of goods, services, and transfers exceed exports. Sustainable threshold for India: ~2.5% to 3.0% of GDP.
Twin Deficit Hypothesis Fiscal Deficit ($G - T$) drives Current Account Deficit ($M - X$) via: $(M - X) = (I - S) + (G - T)$.

11. Foreign Exchange (Forex) & Open-Economy Parities

Purchasing Power Parity (PPP): Absolute: $S = P / P^*$; Relative: $\% \Delta S \approx \pi - \pi^*$
Covered Interest Parity (CIP): $\frac{F}{S} = \frac{1 + i}{1 + i^*} \implies \frac{F - S}{S} \approx i - i^*$ (Forward premium equals interest rate differential)
Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP): $\frac{E(S_{t+1}) - S_t}{S_t} \approx i - i^*$ (Unhedged carry trade exploits UIP failures)
Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER): $\text{REER} = \text{NEER} \times \frac{P_{\text{domestic}}}{P_{\text{foreign}}}$ (RBI 40-currency basket; $>100$ indicates overvaluation)
Marshall-Lerner Condition: Devaluation improves trade balance if and only if $|e_x + e_m| > 1$
J-Curve Effect: Trade balance worsens initially after devaluation before improving as contracts adjust.

Impossible Trinity (Mundell-Fleming Trilemma): A nation cannot simultaneously achieve: (1) Fixed Exchange Rate, (2) Free Capital Mobility, and (3) Independent Monetary Policy. India maintains free capital mobility and independent monetary policy, allowing the Rupee to float under a Managed Float regime.

India Forex Management: Reserves managed by RBI (FCA, Gold, SDR, RTP). Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) allows up to USD 250,000 per financial year per resident individual. FEMA 1999 decriminalized forex violations into civil offenses.

12. Monetary Policy, Fiscal Policy & Public Finance

A. RBI Monetary Policy Framework (Flexible Inflation Targeting)

  • Target: 4.0% headline CPI inflation with a +/- 2% tolerance band (2% to 6%). Failure = outside band for 3 consecutive quarters.
  • Monetary Policy Committee (MPC): 6 members (3 RBI, 3 Central Government experts). Quorum of 4. Governor has casting vote in ties.
  • Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) Corridor (50 bps asymmetric):
    • Ceiling: Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) = Repo + 25 bps.
    • Middle: Policy Repo Rate (benchmark overnight lending rate).
    • Floor: Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) = Repo - 25 bps (uncollateralized liquidity absorption).
  • Reserve Ratios: Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR, maintained on NDTL with RBI, no floor/ceiling); Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR, max 40% ceiling, maintained in G-Secs/cash/gold).

B. Fiscal Policy & The FRBM Framework

Fiscal Deficit: $\text{Total Expenditure} - (\text{Revenue Receipts} + \text{Non-Debt Capital Receipts})$
Revenue Deficit: $\text{Revenue Expenditure} - \text{Revenue Receipts}$
Effective Revenue Deficit: $\text{Revenue Deficit} - \text{Grants for Creation of Capital Assets}$
Primary Deficit: $\text{Fiscal Deficit} - \text{Net Interest Payments}$
N.K. Singh FRBM Committee Targets: Combined Debt-to-GDP ceiling of 60% (40% Centre, 20% States), Central Fiscal Deficit target of 3.0% of GDP, Escape Clause permitting up to 0.5% GDP deviation for national security, war, or natural calamities.

13. Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) & Regulatory Framework

A. Definition & Statutory Base

Regulated under Section 45-I(f) of the RBI Act, 1934. Meets the 50-50 Principal Business Test: Financial assets $> 50\%$ of total assets, and financial income $> 50\%$ of gross income.

B. Differences: Commercial Banks vs. NBFCs

Parameter Commercial Banks NBFCs
Demand Deposits Can accept Demand Deposits (Current and Savings Accounts - CASA). Cannot accept Demand Deposits (only term deposits by deposit-taking NBFCs).
Payment System Part of Payment & Settlement System; can issue cheques drawn on themselves. Cannot issue cheques drawn on themselves; cannot participate in payment clearing.
Deposit Insurance Insured by DICGC up to Rs 5 lakh per depositor. Zero deposit insurance from DICGC.
Reserve Requirements Mandated both CRR and SLR. CRR does not apply; SLR applies to deposit-taking NBFCs.

C. RBI Scale-Based Regulatory (SBR) Framework (Oct 2022)

Layer Coverage / Entities Included Regulatory Stance & Key Norms
Base Layer (NBFC-BL) Non-deposit taking NBFCs with asset size < Rs 1,000 crore; specialized non-credit entities: NBFC-P2P, NBFC-AA, and NOFHC. Lighter regulatory touch; Net Owned Fund (NOF) glide path to Rs 10 crore by March 2027.
Middle Layer (NBFC-ML) All deposit-taking NBFCs (NBFC-D) regardless of size; non-deposit NBFCs with assets ≥ Rs 1,000 crore; HFCs; CICs; IFCs; IDFs; SPDs. Stricter prudential norms, CRAR of 15%, Net NPA PCA framework, enhanced disclosures.
Upper Layer (NBFC-UL) Top 10 NBFCs by asset size and entities selected via parametric scoring (size, interconnectedness, complexity). Bank-like regulation, CET1 capital of 9%, mandatory listing on stock exchanges within 3 years, Large Exposure Framework (LEF).
Top Layer (NBFC-TL) Empty by default; populated if RBI identifies extreme systemic risk in an Upper Layer NBFC. Customized supervisory oversight and bespoke capital surcharges.

D. Specialized NBFC Categories

  • Core Investment Companies (CICs): Holding companies holding ≥ 90% of net assets in group equity/debt (with ≥ 60% in equity shares). Systemically important if assets ≥ Rs 100 crore.
  • Housing Finance Companies (HFCs): Regulated by RBI since 2019. ≥ 60% of total assets must be housing finance, with ≥ 50% in individual retail housing loans.
  • Microfinance (NBFC-MFI): Collateral-free loans to households with annual income up to Rs 3,00,000; monthly debt-servicing outflows capped at 50% of monthly household income.
  • Prompt Corrective Action (PCA): Extended to NBFCs (Middle and Upper layers) with trigger points on CRAR, Tier-1 capital, and Net NPAs (>6%).
