Cost & Management Accounting Overview & Cost Sheet

1.1 Cost Accounting vs. Financial Accounting vs. Management Accounting

Basis Financial Accounting Cost Accounting Management Accounting
Primary Users External users (shareholders, banks, tax authorities, regulators) Internal operational management (supervisors, engineers, cost controllers) Internal strategic management (executives, board of directors)
Time Focus Historical (past financial periods) Past and Present (standard costs, operational actuals) Futuristic (projections, budgets, strategic planning)
Statutory Requirement Mandatory under Companies Act, 2013 Mandatory only for specified classes of companies (Sec 148) Purely voluntary and advisory
Scope of Analysis Macro (entire company as a single reporting entity) Micro (by job, product, process, batch, cost center) Holistic (incorporates financial, cost, and qualitative data)

1.2 Elements of Cost & Cost Sheet Progression

Direct Materials Consumed = Opening Stock of RM + Purchases + Carriage Inward - Returns Outward - Closing Stock of RM
Prime Cost = Direct Materials Consumed + Direct Labor (Wages) + Direct Expenses
Works Cost (Factory Cost) = Prime Cost + Factory Overheads + Opening WIP - Closing WIP
Cost of Production = Works Cost + Quality Control Costs + R&D Costs (Production) + Admin Overheads (Production) - Scrap Realization
Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) = Cost of Production + Opening Finished Goods - Closing Finished Goods
Cost of Sales = COGS + Selling & Distribution Overheads + General Administrative Overheads
Selling Price = Cost of Sales + Net Profit Margin
EXAM TRAP: Non-Cost Financial Appropriations (Strictly EXCLUDED from Cost Sheet)
Interest on debentures / loans, income tax paid, dividend paid or proposed, loss on sale of capital assets, goodwill/preliminary expenses written off, transfer to general reserves, and donations are purely financial appropriations and are EXCLUDED from the Cost Sheet.

1.3 Cost Classifications Master Summary

  • By Traceability:
    • Direct Costs: Easily and economically traced directly to a cost object (e.g. leather in shoe manufacturing, assembly line wages).
    • Indirect Costs (Overheads): Common costs that cannot be directly traced to a single unit (factory rent, machine lubricants, supervisor salary).
  • By Behavior:
    • Fixed Cost: Total remains constant; cost per unit decreases as output expands.
    • Variable Cost: Total varies in direct proportion to output; cost per unit remains constant.
    • Semi-Variable Cost: Contains both fixed and variable elements (e.g. electricity with fixed meter charge + per unit charge). Segregated using High-Low Method: Variable Cost/unit = Δ Cost / Δ Activity.
    • Step-Fixed Cost: Fixed over an activity band, then jumps sharply to a higher plateau (e.g. hiring an additional supervisor after crossing 5,000 units).
  • For Managerial Decision Making:
    • Sunk Cost: Already incurred in the past; irrecoverable; completely irrelevant to future decisions.
    • Opportunity Cost: The benefit foregone by choosing one alternative over the next best alternative.
    • Imputed (Notional) Cost: Hypothetical cost with no actual cash outlay (e.g. interest on proprietary capital, rent of self-owned building).
    • Differential (Incremental) Cost: Difference in total cost between two commercial alternatives.
  • Responsibility Centers: Cost Center (evaluates cost control), Revenue Center (evaluates sales), Profit Center (evaluates revenue & cost), Investment Center (evaluates ROI = Operating Income / Operating Assets & Residual Income).

1.4 Cost Audit (Section 148 of Companies Act, 2013)

  • Form CRA-1: Maintenance of cost records by the company.
  • Form CRA-2: Intimation of appointment of Cost Auditor to Central Government (within 30 days of board meeting or 180 days of FY start).
  • Form CRA-3: Cost Audit Report submitted by Cost Auditor to the Board of Directors (within 180 days of FY close).
  • Form CRA-4: Filing of Cost Audit Report by the company with the Central Government (within 30 days of receiving CRA-3).

Methods of Costing

2.1 Methods of Costing Overview

Method Ideal Industry Application Standard Cost Unit Core Characteristics
Job Costing Printing press, custom engineering, interior design, repair workshops Per individual job / work order Heterogeneous custom output; costs tracked on dedicated Job Cost Cards.
Batch Costing Pharmaceuticals (medicines), biscuits, readymade garments, toy manufacturing Per batch of identical items Economic Batch Quantity (EBQ) balances batch setup cost with inventory holding cost.
Contract Costing Civil construction, bridge construction, shipbuilding, real estate developers Per contract (large-scale, long-duration site project) Retention money, escalation clause, work certified vs uncertified, notional profit.
Process Costing Oil refineries, chemical manufacturing, sugar, cement, textiles, paper mills Per unit of process output (kg, liter, tonne) Continuous mass production of homogeneous products; normal/abnormal loss, equivalent units.
Unit / Output Costing Brick kilns, coal mining, quarries, breweries Per unit (e.g. per 1,000 bricks, per tonne of coal extracted) Single uniform product; prepared via simple element-wise Cost Sheet.
Operating / Service Costing Transport (bus, railway, airline), hospitals, hotels, electricity utilities, canteens Composite units (Passenger-km, Tonne-km, Room-day, Patient-day, kWh) Classified into Standing (Fixed), Maintenance (Semi-variable), and Running (Variable) costs.

2.2 Economic Batch Quantity (EBQ) Formula

EBQ = √ [ (2 × D × S) / C ]
Where: D = Annual Demand; S = Setup Cost per batch; C = Annual Carrying/Holding Cost per unit

2.3 Contract Costing & Notional Profit Rules

Notional Profit = Value of Work Certified + Cost of Work Uncertified - Total Cost Incurred to Date
Retention Money = Value of Work Certified - Cash Received
Degree of Completion (Work Certified / Contract Price) Portion of Notional Profit Transferred to Costing P&L
Less than 25% NIL (No profit recognized; high uncertainty)
25% to less than 50% 1/3 × Notional Profit × (Cash Received / Work Certified)
50% to less than 90% 2/3 × Notional Profit × (Cash Received / Work Certified)
90% or more (Near Completion) Estimated Total Profit × (Work Certified / Contract Price) × (Cash Received / Work Certified)

2.4 Process Costing: Losses & Equivalent Units

  • Normal Loss: Inherent, unavoidable loss under standard operating conditions. Credited to Process Account at Scrap Realization Value. Increases unit cost of good output.
  • Abnormal Loss: Loss exceeding normal allowance due to operational inefficiency/breakdown. Credited to Process Account at Full Normal Cost per unit, debited to Costing P&L (after adjusting scrap).
  • Abnormal Gain: Actual loss is less than expected normal loss. Debited to Process Account at Full Normal Cost per unit, credited to Abnormal Gain Account, net benefit transferred to Costing P&L.
  • Cost per Unit Formula:
    Cost per Unit = (Total Process Cost - Scrap Value of Normal Loss) / (Input Units - Normal Loss Units)
  • Equivalent Units Methods:
    • FIFO Method: Work needed to complete opening WIP + Units started & completed + Closing WIP.
    • Weighted Average Method: Units completed & transferred + Closing WIP equivalent units (pools opening costs with current costs).

2.5 Joint Products & By-Products

  • Joint Cost Allocation Methods: (1) Physical Units Method; (2) Sales Value at Split-off Point; (3) Net Realizable Value (NRV) Method [Final Sales Value - Separable Further Processing Costs - Selling Expenses]; (4) Constant Gross Margin Percentage NRV Method.
  • Further Processing Decision Rule: A joint product should be processed beyond split-off ONLY if: Incremental Revenue from Further Processing > Incremental Further Processing Cost. Joint costs are sunk and irrelevant!
  • By-Product Treatment: Net Realizable Value (or standard cost) of by-product is credited to the main process account to reduce the net cost of the main product.

2.6 Service Costing: Transport Formulae

Total Passenger-Kilometers = Number of Buses × Trips/Day × Distance (km) × Days in Month × Seating Capacity × Occupancy %
Absolute Tonne-Km (Weighted Average) = Σ (Weight Carried × Distance for each journey leg)
Commercial Tonne-Km (Simple Average) = Total Distance Run × Average Load Carried

Cost Control & Decision Making (Standard, Marginal, Budgetary)

3.1 Standard Costing & Variance Analysis Formulas

MATERIAL VARIANCES:
• Material Cost Variance (MCV) = (SQ × SP) - (AQ × AP)
• Material Price Variance (MPV) = AQ × (SP - AP)
• Material Usage Variance (MUV) = SP × (SQ - AQ)
• Material Mix Variance (MMV) = Σ [SP × (Revised Standard Quantity - Actual Quantity)]
• Material Yield Variance (MYV) = Standard Output Cost/unit × (Actual Yield - Standard Yield)
Verification: MCV = MPV + MUV = MPV + MMV + MYV
LABOR VARIANCES:
• Labor Cost Variance (LCV) = (SH × SR) - (AH × AR)
• Labor Rate Variance (LRV) = AH × (SR - AR)
• Labor Efficiency Variance (LEV) = SR × (SH - Actual Hours Worked)
• Idle Time Variance (ITV) = Abnormal Idle Hours × SR (ALWAYS ADVERSE!)
Verification: LCV = LRV + LEV + ITV
OVERHEAD VARIANCES:
• Fixed Overhead Expenditure (Budget) Variance = Budgeted Fixed OH - Actual Fixed OH
• Fixed Overhead Volume Variance = Absorbed Fixed OH - Budgeted Fixed OH
• Fixed Overhead Capacity Variance = Standard Rate × (Actual Hours - Budgeted Hours)
• Fixed Overhead Calendar Variance = Standard Rate/day × (Actual Days - Budgeted Days)

3.2 Marginal Costing & Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) Analysis

Marginal Cost Equation: Sales - Variable Cost = Contribution = Fixed Cost + Profit
Profit-Volume (P/V) Ratio = (Contribution / Sales) × 100 = (Δ Profit / Δ Sales) × 100 = 100% - Variable Cost Ratio
Break-Even Point (in units) = Total Fixed Cost / Contribution per unit
Break-Even Point (in value) = Total Fixed Cost / P/V Ratio
Margin of Safety (MOS) = Actual Sales - Break-Even Sales
Margin of Safety Ratio = (MOS / Actual Sales) × 100 = Profit / (Actual Sales × P/V Ratio)
Sales for Target Profit = (Total Fixed Cost + Desired Profit) / P/V Ratio
Sales for Target Profit After Tax = [ Total Fixed Cost + { Desired PAT / (1 - t) } ] / P/V Ratio
Cash Break-Even Point = Cash Fixed Costs (excluding depreciation) / P/V Ratio
Indifference Point (Cost BEP) = Δ Fixed Costs / Δ Variable Cost per unit
Shut-Down Point = Avoidable (Escapable) Fixed Costs / P/V Ratio

3.3 Absorption Costing vs. Marginal Costing

Scenario Relationship between Net Profits Underlying Accounting Reason
Production = Sales Absorption Profit = Marginal Profit No change in inventory levels; identical fixed overheads expensed.
Production > Sales
(Inventory Increases)		 Absorption Profit > Marginal Profit Fixed production overheads are capitalized in closing inventory under Absorption Costing, deferring expenses to next year.
Production < Sales
(Inventory Decreases)		 Absorption Profit < Marginal Profit Opening inventory releases previous year's capitalized fixed overheads into current COGS under Absorption Costing.

3.4 Budgetary Control & Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)

  • Principal Budget Factor (Key Factor): The factor that limits or constrains the volume of output (sales demand, raw material supply, machine capacity). All functional budgets depend on it.
  • Functional Budget Flow: Sales Budget → Production Budget → Direct Materials / Labor / Overheads Budgets → Cash Budget → Master Budget (Budgeted P&L and Balance Sheet).
  • Production Budget Formula: Budgeted Sales + Desired Closing FG - Opening FG.
  • Materials Purchase Budget Formula: Materials Required for Production + Desired Closing RM - Opening RM.
  • Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB): Pioneered by Peter Pyhrr. Eliminates incremental budgeting; starts from a zero baseline every cycle.
    4 Core Steps: 1. Identify Decision Units → 2. Formulate Decision Packages → 3. Evaluate & Rank Decision Packages → 4. Allocate Resources based on priority.
  • Budget Control Ratios:
    Capacity Ratio = (Actual Hours / Budgeted Hours) × 100
    Efficiency Ratio = (Standard Hours for Actual Output / Actual Hours) × 100
    Activity Ratio = (Standard Hours for Actual Output / Budgeted Hours) × 100
    Relationship: Activity Ratio = Capacity Ratio × Efficiency Ratio

Lean System & Innovation

4.1 Just-in-Time (JIT) & Lean Manufacturing

  • Core Philosophy: A demand-pull system eliminating all forms of non-value-adding waste (Muda), aiming for zero inventory, zero defects, zero machine breakdowns, and batch size of one.
  • Kanban System: Visual replenishment signaling mechanism (signboards/cards). Production Kanban: authorizes workstation to make parts; Withdrawal Kanban: authorizes moving parts between stations.
  • Takt Time: The pace of production required to match customer demand:
    Takt Time = Net Available Operating Production Time / Customer Demand in Units
  • Backflush Costing: Simplified cost accounting in JIT. Skips detailed stage-by-stage tracking of WIP; delays cost recording until completion or sale, flushing costs backward using bills of materials.
  • The 3Ms of Toyota Production System: (1) Muda (Waste); (2) Muri (Overburden); (3) Mura (Unevenness / Inconsistency).
  • The 7 Wastes (TIMWOOD): Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Overproduction (the worst waste), Overprocessing, and Defects.

4.2 Kaizen Costing & 5S Workplace Methodology

  • Kaizen Costing: Continuous, incremental improvement during the operational manufacturing stage, driven by front-line workers (Kaizen Teian suggestion schemes and Gemba visits).
  • The 5S Methodology:
    1. Seiri (Sort): Separate necessary items from unnecessary items; apply Red Tagging to remove obsolete clutter.
    2. Seiton (Set in Order): 'A place for everything and everything in its place'; visual shadow boards, floor markings.
    3. Seiso (Shine / Sweep): Cleaning as an inspection mechanism to detect leaks, wear, and loose bolts early.
    4. Seiketsu (Standardize): Establish uniform visual procedures and schedules to maintain the first 3Ss.
    5. Shitsuke (Sustain / Self-Discipline): Embed 5S habits into the organizational culture through audits and scorecards.

4.3 Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) & OEE

  • Core Pillars: Jishu Hozen (Autonomous Maintenance by operators), Kobetsu Kaizen (Focused Improvement), Planned Maintenance, Quality Maintenance, Training & Safety.
  • Six Big Losses: (1) Breakdowns; (2) Setup/Adjustments; (3) Idling/Minor Stops; (4) Reduced Speed; (5) Defects/Rework; (6) Startup/Yield Losses.
  • Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE):
    OEE = Availability Rate × Performance Rate × Quality Rate
    Availability = Run Time / Planned Operating Time
    Performance = (Ideal Cycle Time × Total Output) / Run Time
    Quality = Good Output / Total Output Started
    World-Class Benchmark: OEE ≥ 85% (Availability ≥ 90%, Performance ≥ 95%, Quality ≥ 99%)

4.4 Six Sigma Quality Management

  • Statistical Benchmark: No more than 3.4 Defects Per Million Opportunities (DPMO) (99.99966% defect-free), allowing for a 1.5-sigma long-term mean shift.
  • Methodologies:
    • DMAIC (Existing Processes): Define → Measure → Analyze → Improve → Control.
    • DMADV (New Processes / DFSS): Define → Measure → Analyze → Design → Verify.
  • Six Sigma Belt Roles: Champions (Executive sponsors) → Master Black Belts (Trainers/mentors) → Black Belts (Full-time project leaders) → Green Belts (Part-time project leads) → Yellow/White Belts (Team members).
  • Cost of Quality (COQ):
    • Conformity Costs: Prevention (training, design reviews) + Appraisal (testing, inspection).
    • Non-Conformity Costs: Internal Failure (scrap, rework) + External Failure (warranty claims, recalls).
  • Taguchi Loss Function: Economic loss to society begins as soon as a characteristic deviates from the nominal target value, increasing quadratically: L(y) = k(y - m)².

4.5 Business Process Reengineering (BPR) & Target Costing

  • BPR (Hammer & Champy): Fundamental rethinking and radical redesign of core business processes from a clean slate to achieve quantum leaps in performance (cost, quality, speed, service), replacing assembly-line fragmentation with empowered Case Managers enabled by information technology.
  • Target Costing: Market-driven costing applied upstream during the product design phase:
    Target Cost = Competitive Target Selling Price - Desired Target Profit Margin
    Engineers apply Value Engineering (VE) to eliminate costs without sacrificing function or quality.
  • Life Cycle Costing: Estimates total costs from 'cradle to grave' (R&D, design, manufacturing, operations, maintenance, decommissioning, and environmental disposal). 70% to 80% of lifetime costs are committed during the initial design phase!
