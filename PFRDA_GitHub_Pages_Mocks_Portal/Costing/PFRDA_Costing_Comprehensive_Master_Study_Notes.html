Comprehensive Master Study Notes: Subject F — Cost & Management Accounting

1. Cost & Management Accounting Overview, Cost Concepts & Cost Sheet

Cost Accounting is a quantitative technique that records, classifies, allocates, and analyzes expenditure for determining product cost, exercising cost control, and aiding managerial decision-making.

Cost of Production (COP) + Opening FG − Closing FG + Selling & Distribution OH = COST OF SALES

Cost Classification Principles

Classification Basis Categories Significance & Exam Traps
By Traceability Direct Costs vs. Indirect Costs Direct costs physically traceable to cost object; indirect costs pooled as Overheads.
By Behavior Fixed, Variable, Semi-Variable Fixed cost per unit varies inversely with volume; variable cost per unit remains constant!
For Decision-Making Sunk, Opportunity, Relevant, Marginal, Differential, Discretionary Costs Sunk costs (historical past cash outflows) are strictly irrelevant for future decisions. Opportunity cost = return foregone on next best alternative.

2. Methods of Costing: Unit, Job, Batch, Contract, Process & Service

Costing Method Target Industries & Applicability Core Mechanism & Accounting Treatment
Unit / Single Costing Collieries, cement, brickworks, steel rolling mills. Identical, continuous, uniform output. Cost per unit = Total Cost / Total Units.
Job Costing Printing presses, machine tool repair, interior design. Cost collected separately for each customized job using Job Cost Cards.
Batch Costing Pharmaceuticals, garments, spare parts manufacturing. Economic Batch Quantity (EBQ) formula: EBQ = √[(2 × D × S) / C] where S = Setting up cost, C = Carrying cost per unit per annum.
Contract Costing Construction, ship building, civil infrastructure. Long duration jobs. Retention Money = Value of Work Certified − Cash Received from Contractee. Architectural certification of completion.
Process Costing Chemical refineries, sugar, textiles, paper. Continuous sequence of manufacturing processes. Normal Loss absorbed into good units cost; Abnormal Loss/Gain transferred to Costing P&L. Equivalent Units = Finished Units + (Closing WIP × % Completion).
Service / Operating Costing Transport, hospitals, hotels, power generation. Composite cost units: Passenger-Kilometers, Ton-Kilometers, Patient-Days, Room-Days, Kilowatt-Hours.

3. Cost Control: Marginal Costing, Standard Costing & Budgetary Control

Marginal Costing (CVP Analysis) & Break-Even Mechanics

Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost = Fixed Cost + Profit
Profit-Volume (P/V) Ratio = (Contribution / Sales) × 100 = (ΔProfit / ΔSales) × 100
Break-Even Point (Units) = Fixed Cost / Contribution per Unit
Break-Even Point (Value) = Fixed Cost / P/V Ratio
Margin of Safety (MoS) = Actual Sales − Break-Even Sales = Profit / P/V Ratio
Sales for Desired Profit = (Fixed Cost + Desired Profit) / P/V Ratio
Shut-Down Point (Units) = Avoidable Fixed Costs / Contribution per Unit

Standard Costing Variance Analysis Master Formulas

Variance Category Standard Formula Interpretation
Material Cost Variance (MCV) (SQ × SP) − (AQ × AP) Total material variance. MCV = MPV + MUV.
Material Price Variance (MPV) AQ × (SP − AP) Responsibility of Purchase Manager. Evaluated on Actual Quantity bought/used.
Material Usage Variance (MUV) SP × (SQ − AQ) Responsibility of Production Manager. Evaluated at Standard Price.
Labour Cost Variance (LCV) (SH × SR) − (AH × AR) Total labor variance. LCV = LRV + LEV + ITV.
Labour Rate Variance (LRV) AH × (SR − AR) Wage rate differentials paid to workers.
Labour Efficiency Variance (LEV) SR × (SH − AH_worked) Worker productivity efficiency evaluated at Standard Rate.
Idle Time Variance (ITV) Idle Hours × SR Always Adverse (Unfavorable)! Arises due to power cuts, breakdowns.

Lean thinking eliminates waste (Muda), irregularity (Mura), and overburden (Muri) to optimize customer value with minimal resources.

5. Complete Cost Accounting Standards (CAS 1–24), Process Equivalent Units & Numerical Masterclass

5.1 Process Costing: Equivalent Production under FIFO vs. Weighted Average Method (WAM)

When partially completed Work-in-Progress (WIP) exists at the beginning and end of a period: under the FIFO Method, only the additional degree of work performed in the current period on Opening WIP is included in equivalent units, and Opening WIP costs are kept separate and added directly to completed units. Under the Weighted Average Method (WAM), Opening WIP units are merged with current period production (100% equivalent units for all completed output), and Opening WIP cost is pooled with current period cost by element (Material, Labour, Overheads) to compute a single weighted average cost per equivalent unit.

Abnormal Loss vs. Abnormal Gain Valuation: Normal loss is absorbed by good units (reducing the denominator of normal output and crediting normal scrap realizable value to the Process Account). Both Abnormal Loss and Abnormal Gain are always valued at the full cost per normal unit: Cost per Normal Unit = (Total Process Cost − Scrap Value of Normal Loss) ÷ (Total Input Units − Normal Loss Units). Note that in the Abnormal Gain Account, the foregone scrap value of normal loss on the abnormal gain units is debited before transferring net abnormal gain to the Costing P&L Account.

5.2 Joint Products & By-Products Apportionment Methods

  • Physical Units Method: Joint costs up to the split-off point are apportioned in proportion to physical weight, volume, or output units.
  • Sales Value at Split-Off Point Method: Joint costs are allocated based on the relative market value of each joint product exactly at the split-off point.
  • Net Realizable Value (NRV) Method: Used when products are processed further after split-off: NRV = Final Sales Value after Further Processing − Post-Split-Off Further Processing Costs − Selling & Distribution Expenses.
  • Constant Gross Margin Percentage NRV Method: Allocates joint costs so that every joint product earns the exact same overall gross margin percentage on final sales.
