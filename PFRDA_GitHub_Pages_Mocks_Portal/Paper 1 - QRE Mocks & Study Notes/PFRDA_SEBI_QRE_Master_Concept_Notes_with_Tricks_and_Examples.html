⚡ 1. Speed Math & Calculation Shortcuts

Speed calculation forms the backbone of regulatory paper clearing. Memorizing percentage-fraction conversions cuts Data Interpretation solving time by over 50%.

💡 Pro-Tricks & Shortcuts:
  • Squaring Numbers Ending in 5: Multiply the leading digits by (digits + 1) and append 25. e.g., 65² = (6 × 7) | 25 = 4225. 115² = (11 × 12) | 25 = 13225.
  • Multiplication by 11: To multiply a two-digit number ab × 11, sum the digits in between: a | (a+b) | b. e.g., 43 × 11 = 473. 78 × 11 = 7 | (15) | 8 = 858.
  • Percentage Splitting Technique: Calculate 54% of 750 = 50% (375) + 4% (30) = 405. Or interchange: x% of y = y% of x. (e.g., 68% of 50 = 50% of 68 = 34).

Problem: In a regulatory exam DI set, evaluate: 37.5% of 640 + 57.14% of 490 - 83.33% of 240.

Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Convert percentages to exact fractions: 37.5% = 3/8, 57.14% = 4/7, 83.33% = 5/6.

3. = (3 × 80) + (4 × 70) - (5 × 40) = 240 + 280 - 200 = 320. (Solved mentally in 10 seconds!)

📐 2. Approximations & Simplifications

Approximations consistently provide 5 questions in SEBI and PFRDA Phase 1 Paper 1. They are designed to test your estimation skills rather than exact pen-and-paper arithmetic.

💡 Pro-Tricks & Rules:
  • Round to Nearest Benchmark: Decimal .89, .95 round UP (e.g. 419.82 → 420; 2024.95 → 2025). Decimal .02, .12 round DOWN.
  • Strict BODMAS Order: Brackets → Orders (Roots/Powers) → Division & Multiplication → Addition & Subtraction. Always perform division before large multiplications.
  • Denominator Rounding: When dividing by ~6.94, round to 7. When dividing by ~4.02, round to 4.
Worked Example: Multi-Operator Approximation SEBI 2025 PYQ Style

Question: What approximate value will come in place of (?)?
49.88% of 799.91 + 24.93% of 1199.85 = ? % of 1399.78

Solution:
• Round values: 50% of 800 + 25% of 1200 ≈ ? % of 1400
• LHS = 400 + 300 = 700.
• RHS = (? / 100) × 1400 = 14 × ?
• ? = 700 / 14 = 50.

🎯 3. Quadratic Equations: The 15-Second Sign Method

For standard quadratic equations ax² + bx + c = 0, roots can be determined rapidly by inspecting the signs of the linear coefficient (b) and constant (c).

Equation TypeSign of bSign of cRoot 1 SignRoot 2 SignShortcut Rule
x² - bx + c = 0-+++Both roots are always POSITIVE.
x² + bx + c = 0++--Both roots are always NEGATIVE.
x² - bx - c = 0--+-Larger root is POSITIVE, Smaller is NEGATIVE.
x² + bx - c = 0+--+Larger root is NEGATIVE, Smaller is POSITIVE.
💡 Instant Comparison Rules (Zero-Calculation):
  1. If Eq I has (+, +) signs and Eq II has (-, +) signs, then without solving: x < y (all roots of I are negative, all roots of II are positive).
  2. If the constant c is negative in both equations (c < 0), roots for both are (+, -). Hence, Relationship Cannot Be Established (CND) holds true 100% of the time!
Worked Example: Quadratic Root Comparison PFRDA 2025 Recollected

Question: Solve both equations and establish the relationship:
I. x² - 18x + 77 = 0
II. y² - 25y + 154 = 0

Solution:
• Eq I: Signs (-, +) => Both roots (+, +). Factors of 77 adding to 18: 11 × 7 = 77, 11 + 7 = 18. Roots: x = 7, 11.
• Eq II: Signs (-, +) => Both roots (+, +). Factors of 154 adding to 25: 14 × 11 = 154, 14 + 11 = 25. Roots: y = 11, 14.
• Comparison:
- x = 7 is < 11 and < 14.
- x = 11 is = 11 and < 14.
• Conclusion: x ≤ y.

🔢 4. Number Series Patterns & Detection Algorithms

Pattern 1: Differences of Squares / Cubes

Differences are squares or cubes: +9, +25, +49 (3², 5², 7² primes) or +8, +27, +64 (2³, 3³, 4³).

Pattern 2: Multiplicative + Constant

×1+1, ×2+2, ×3+3, ×4+4... or ×2-1, ×2-2, ×2-3...

Pattern 3: Decimal 0.5 Multiplier

×0.5 + 1, ×1 + 1.5, ×1.5 + 2, ×2 + 2.5... (First two terms are almost identical or decrease slightly, then grow rapidly).

Pattern 4: Alternating Double Series

Interleaved operations: +k, -m, +k, -m... or square additions and subtractions: +2², -3², +4², -5².

Worked Example: Wrong Number Detection SEBI Grade A Pattern

Series: 12, 14, 32, 102, 414, 2090, 12570. Find the wrong number.

Step-by-Step Logic:
• 12 × 1 + 2 = 14
• 14 × 2 + 4 = 32
• 32 × 3 + 6 = 102
• 102 × 4 + 8 = 416 (Given term is 414!)
• Verify next: 416 × 5 + 10 = 2080 + 10 = 2090 (Matches!).
• Hence, 414 is the wrong number (should be 416).

📊 5. Arithmetic Core Formulas with Worked Examples

A. Simple & Compound Interest Difference

• 2-Year Difference (CI - SI): D₂ = P × (R / 100)² • 3-Year Difference (CI - SI): D₃ = P × (R / 100)² × [ 3 + (R / 100) ]
Worked Example: 2-Year CI-SI Difference Exam Standard

Problem: The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a principal at 12% per annum for 2 years is Rs. 432. Find the principal sum.

Solution using formula:
D₂ = P × (R / 100)² => 432 = P × (12 / 100)² = P × (144 / 10000)
P = (432 × 10000) / 144 = 3 × 10000 = Rs. 30,000.

B. Partnerships with Working Partner Deductions

• Profit Ratio = (Capital_A × Time_A) : (Capital_B × Time_B) : (Capital_C × Time_C) • Working Partner Salary: Subtract management salary from Total Profit FIRST, then distribute remaining amount.
Worked Example: Working Partner Salary Deduction Regulatory Classic

Problem: A and B invest Rs. 50,000 and Rs. 70,000 in a business. A is an active working partner and receives 10% of total profit as management salary. If the total annual profit is Rs. 40,000, find A's total income.

Solution:
1. A's salary = 10% of 40,000 = Rs. 4,000.
2. Remaining profit = 40,000 - 4,000 = Rs. 36,000.
3. Investment ratio A : B = 50,000 : 70,000 = 5 : 7. Total parts = 12.
4. A's profit share = (5 / 12) × 36,000 = Rs. 15,000.
5. A's total income = Salary + Profit Share = 4,000 + 15,000 = Rs. 19,000.

C. Mixtures & Repeated Dilution

• Repeated Replacement: Remaining Pure Liquid = Initial_Qty × (1 - x / V)ⁿ where V = total volume, x = volume replaced each operation, n = number of operations.
Worked Example: Repeated Liquid Replacement Shortcut Formula

Problem: A container has 80 liters of pure milk. 8 liters of milk are taken out and replaced with water. This process is repeated 2 more times (total 3 times). How much pure milk remains?

Solution:
Remaining Milk = 80 × (1 - 8/80)³ = 80 × (1 - 0.1)³ = 80 × (0.9)³
= 80 × 0.729 = 58.32 liters.

D. Boats and Streams

• Downstream (D) = u + v | Upstream (U) = u - v • Boat Speed in Still Water (u) = (D + U) / 2 • Stream Speed (v) = (D - U) / 2
Worked Example: Stream and Boat Speed Speed Calculation

Problem: A boat travels 48 km downstream in 3 hours and 32 km upstream in 4 hours. Find the speed of the river current.

Solution:
• Downstream Speed D = 48 / 3 = 16 km/h.
• Upstream Speed U = 32 / 4 = 8 km/h.
• Current Speed v = (D - U) / 2 = (16 - 8) / 2 = 4 km/h.

📈 6. Data Interpretation (DI) Speed Toolkit

💡 High-Speed DI Shortcuts:
  • Percentage Increase/Decrease: (Difference / Initial Value) × 100%. When comparing growth across 4 companies, compare the fractions directly without multiplying by 100.
  • Cross-Multiplication for Fraction Comparison: To compare 45/112 and 52/128, cross multiply: 45 × 128 = 5760; 52 × 112 = 5824. Since 5824 > 5760, 52/128 is larger!
  • Average of Group: Pick an assumed mean (e.g. 500) and calculate average deviation, then adjust.

Worked Example: "Only a few" with Possibility SEBI 2024 Exam Pattern

Statements:
Only a few Bonds are Equities.
All Equities are Shares.
No Shares are Debentures.
Conclusions:
I. Some Bonds are not Debentures.
II. All Equities being Debentures is a possibility.

Solution:
• Equities are inside Shares. No Shares are Debentures => No Equities can be Debentures. Therefore, Conclusion II is 100% False.
• Since some Bonds are Equities and all Equities are Shares (which cannot be Debentures), that portion of Bonds can never be Debentures. Conclusion I is Definitely True.
• Answer: Only Conclusion I follows.

⚖️ 8. Inequalities: Open Gate Framework

Open Gate Mechanics:
  • Symbol > represents an open door from left to right. You can walk from A to B in A > B.
  • For A ≥ B to be valid, EVERY gate along the path must have an equal sign (≥ or =). If even one strict gate (>) appears, the relation strictly reduces to A > B.
  • If opposing arrows meet (e.g. A > B < C), the door is blocked. No definite conclusion exists.
Worked Example: Multi-Statement Inequality PFRDA Exam Standard

Statements: P ≥ Q > R = S; S ≥ T > U
Conclusions:
I. P > T
II. Q > U

Solution:
• Path from P to T: P ≥ Q > R = S ≥ T. Notice strict gate '>' exists. Hence P > T is True.
• Path from Q to U: Q > R = S ≥ T > U. Strict gate exists. Hence Q > U is True.
• Answer: Both conclusions follow.

🏢 9. Puzzles & Seating Arrangements: 4-Phase Algorithm

Phase 1: Scanning & Triage

Spend 20 seconds counting variables. Solve single-variable linear rows or 8-floor buildings first; leave dual-variable month-date puzzles for later.

Phase 2: Anchor Identification

Find definite anchor points: "X sits at an extreme end", "Y lives on Floor 8", or "Z sits third to the right of corner person".

Phase 3: Two-Case Parallel Grids

Always draw Case 1 and Case 2 side-by-side. Never hold branching scenarios in your head. Enter definite clues on both grids simultaneously.

Phase 4: Negative Clue Elimination

Use negative clues ("Neither A nor B likes Blue", "C does not sit adjacent to D") to eliminate invalid cases cleanly.

🧠 10. Critical Reasoning: The Assumption Negation Test

The Golden Negation Rule:

An assumption is an unstated foundation. If you negate an option (make it opposite) and the conclusion completely collapses, that option is 100% an implicit assumption!

Worked Example: Assumption Testing Regulatory Decision Making

Statement: The central bank has mandated that all Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) maintain higher capital adequacy buffers to mitigate retail credit risk.
Assumption I: Higher capital buffers protect financial institutions during sudden default shocks.

Negation Test:
Negate Assumption I: "Higher capital buffers DO NOT protect institutions during default shocks."
If buffers do not protect them, why would the central bank mandate them? The policy becomes completely pointless! Thus, the conclusion collapses upon negation.
Therefore, Assumption I is Implicit.

📖 11. 40 Golden Grammar Rules for Error Detection (with Worked Examples)

Rule 1: Along with / Together with

Verb agrees with the FIRST subject.
Incorrect: The CEO along with his directors are attending.
Correct: The CEO along with his directors is attending.

Rule 2: Neither... Nor Agreement

Verb agrees with the NEAREST subject.
Incorrect: Neither the manager nor the employees was present.
Correct: Neither the manager nor the employees were present.

Rule 3: Inversion with Negative Adverbs

Negative adverbs at sentence start require auxiliary inversion.
Incorrect: Hardly the circular had been published...
Correct: Hardly had the circular been published...

Rule 4: Correlative Pairs

• Hardly / Scarcely is paired with when (never 'than').
• No sooner is paired with than (never 'when').
• Not only is paired with but also.

Rule 5: Uncountable Noun Pluralization

Words like information, furniture, luggage, equipment, advice, machinery never take plural 's'. Say pieces of equipment, not equipments.

Rule 6: Absolute Adjectives

Adjectives like unique, perfect, supreme, fatal, ideal cannot take degrees of comparison. Never say very unique or more perfect.

Rule 7: Preposition + Gerund

A verb following a preposition must be in the gerund (V+ing) form.
Incorrect: He was penalized for violate the rules.
Correct: He was penalized for violating the rules.

Rule 8: Redundancy Errors

Never say discussed about, revert back, return back, or supposing if.

Rule 9: Between vs Among

Between is for two distinct entities; Among is for groups of three or more.

Rule 10: One of the + Plural Noun

One of the officers was selected. BUT if followed by a relative pronoun: He is one of the officers who were selected.

Parajumble / Sentence Rearrangement Framework

💡 3-Step Parajumble Solving Framework:
  1. Identify the Independent Opening Sentence: Look for a sentence introducing a broad noun, concept, or historical premise. Sentences starting with However, Therefore, Furthermore, This, Such can NEVER be sentence 1!
  2. Form Mandatory Pairs: Link a full noun to its subsequent pronoun (e.g. The Reserve Bank of India → It / The central bank). Link problem statements to their resulting solutions.
