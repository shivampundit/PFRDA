Accounting as a Financial Information System & Principles

1.1 Fundamental Accounting Assumptions (AS 1)

  • Going Concern: Enterprise is viewed as continuing in operation for the foreseeable future. Liquidation values are ignored; historical cost basis is justified.
  • Consistency: Accounting policies are followed consistently from one period to another to ensure comparability. Change allowed only if: (i) required by law/statute, (ii) required for AS compliance, or (iii) results in more appropriate presentation.
  • Accrual: Revenues and costs are recognized as they are earned or incurred (not as money is received or paid) and recorded in the period to which they relate.
  • Rule: If these 3 assumptions are followed, NO specific disclosure is required. If NOT followed, specific disclosure is MANDATORY.

1.2 Accounting Concepts & Conventions Master Table

Concept / Convention Core Theoretical Meaning Exam Practical Application
Business Entity Business is distinct and separate from its owners/proprietors. Proprietor's capital is a liability; owner's personal expenses debited to Drawings Account.
Money Measurement Only transactions capable of being expressed in monetary terms are recorded. Employee morale, management skill, brand loyalty are excluded from balance sheets.
Periodicity (Time Period) Indefinite life of enterprise is split into artificial regular time intervals. Preparation of annual financial statements, quarterly interim reporting.
Historical Cost Assets are recorded at their original acquisition price. Depreciation charged on acquisition cost; ignores inflation unless revalued under AS 10.
Dual Aspect (Duality) Every transaction has a dual effect: Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity. Basis of double-entry bookkeeping; debits always equal credits.
Matching Principle Expenses incurred to generate revenue of a period must be matched in that period. Recognition of outstanding expenses, prepaid expenses, and unearned revenue.
Prudence (Conservatism) Do not anticipate profits, but provide for all possible future losses. Valuation of inventory at lower of cost or NRV; provision for bad & doubtful debts.
Substance over Form Transactions are recorded based on their financial and economic reality, not merely legal form. Capitalization of assets acquired under finance lease / hire purchase.
Materiality Only items that meaningfully influence economic decisions of users need strict accounting. Expensing low-cost durable assets (staplers, paperweights) immediately to P&L.

1.3 Capital vs. Revenue Expenditure: Master Checklist

  • Capital Expenditure: Benefits multiple accounting periods; increases earning capacity or efficiency beyond originally assessed standards; brings asset to intended operational condition/location. Examples: Machine purchase, freight inwards on machinery, major overhaul before putting second-hand truck to use, temporary shed for factory construction, legal costs to acquire clear title.
  • Revenue Expenditure: Benefits current accounting period only; maintains existing earning capacity in normal operating condition. Examples: Annual maintenance/tune-ups, repairs & whitewashing, legal fees to defend existing title against trespassers, road tax and annual insurance.
  • Deferred Revenue Expenditure: Heavy revenue expense whose benefit spans a few years. Under modern AS 26 / Ind AS 38, advertising and brand launch campaigns must be expensed in P&L as incurred (cannot be carried as intangible assets).

1.4 Rectification of Errors & Trial Balance Agreement

Error Category Trial Balance Balances? Examples & Rectification Approach
Error of Complete Omission YES (Balances) Transaction omitted from both debit and credit. Rectification: Record the journal entry normally.
Error of Principle YES (Balances) Violates capital vs revenue rules (e.g., machinery purchased debited to Purchases). Rectification: Debit Machinery, Credit Purchases.
Compensating Error YES (Balances) Two or more independent errors cancel each other out arithmetically.
Error of Commission (Two-sided) YES (Balances) Wrong amount entered in primary subsidiary book and posted to both accounts equally.
One-Sided Errors NO (Imbalance) Undercast/overcast of subsidiary books; posting to wrong side or wrong amount in one ledger account. Rectification involves Suspense Account.
Prior Period Errors Detected after year-end Rectified in next FY via Profit & Loss Adjustment Account to protect current year's operating results.

Accounting Standards (AS & Ind AS) & Depreciation

2.1 AS 2 / Ind AS 2: Valuation of Inventories

Valuation Rule: Lower of Cost and Net Realizable Value (NRV)
NRV = Estimated Selling Price in Ordinary Course - (Estimated Completion Costs + Estimated Costs to Sell)
  • Cost Formulas: FIFO (First-In, First-Out) or Weighted Average. LIFO is strictly PROHIBITED under both AS 2 and Ind AS 2.
  • Specific Identification Method: Mandatory for items not ordinarily interchangeable and goods/services segregated for specific projects.
  • Exclusions from Cost of Inventory: Abnormal wastage of materials/labor, storage costs (unless necessary before next stage), general administrative overheads, selling and distribution costs (e.g., freight outwards).
  • Raw Materials Valuation Exception: Materials held for use in production are NOT written down below cost if the finished product is expected to be sold at or above cost, even if replacement cost has dropped!
  • By-Products: Immaterial by-products are measured at NRV, which is deducted from the cost of the main product.
  • Ind AS 2 Difference: Ind AS 2 mandates reversal of write-downs if circumstances change; AS 2 does not contain explicit provisions for reversal.

2.2 AS 9 / Ind AS 115: Revenue Recognition

  • Sale of Goods: Revenue recognized when significant risks and rewards of ownership are transferred to the buyer, and no significant uncertainty exists regarding consideration.
  • Rendering of Services: Recognized under Percentage-of-Completion Method (POCM) or Completed Service Contract method based on performance.
  • Interest, Royalties, Dividends:
    • Interest: Recognized on time-proportion basis taking into account effective yield.
    • Royalties: Recognized on accrual basis in accordance with terms of the agreement.
    • Dividends: Recognized when the shareholder's right to receive payment is established.
  • Bill and Hold Sales: Recognized before delivery only if goods are identified, ready, customer requests delay, and usual payment terms apply.
  • Consignment Sales: Recognized only when consignee sells goods to a third party.
  • Ind AS 115 5-Step Model: 1. Identify contract → 2. Identify performance obligations → 3. Determine transaction price → 4. Allocate transaction price → 5. Recognize revenue when/as obligation is satisfied.

2.3 AS 10 / Ind AS 16: Property, Plant and Equipment (PPE)

  • Initial Recognition: Purchase price + Import duties + Directly attributable costs (site prep, installation, initial delivery, testing) + Initial estimate of dismantling and site restoration costs (with corresponding provision).
  • Exclusions from Initial Cost: Costs of opening new facility, advertising/promotional costs, administration and general overheads, staff training costs.
  • Subsequent Expenditure: Capitalized ONLY if it increases future economic benefits beyond originally assessed standards (e.g. major component replacement); routine servicing/repairs are expensed.
  • Component Accounting: MANDATORY. Each significant part of PPE with a differing useful life must be depreciated separately (e.g., aircraft fuselage vs. jet engine).
  • Revaluation Accounting:
    • First Revaluation Upward: Credit to Revaluation Surplus (OCI/Reserves).
    • Subsequent Downward: First debit Revaluation Surplus to reverse existing credit, excess to P&L.
    • Disposal of Revalued Asset: Remaining revaluation surplus transferred to General Reserve / Retained Earnings (never to P&L).
  • Changes in Depreciation Method or Useful Life: Treated as a change in accounting estimate applied PROSPECTIVELY.
  • Bearer Plants: Living plants used solely to bear produce for >1 period (tea bushes, rubber trees) are accounted for as PPE under AS 10 / Ind AS 16. Produce on bearer plants is covered by Ind AS 41.

2.4 AS 11 / Ind AS 21: Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates

Classification Definition & Examples Balance Sheet Translation & Gain/Loss Treatment
Monetary Items Units of currency held, receivables, payables, loans in fixed/determinable amounts. Translated at Closing Rate. Exchange differences recognized in Profit & Loss.
Non-Monetary at Historical Cost Fixed assets, inventories, investments in equity shares. Translated at Exchange Rate on the Date of Transaction (no subsequent exchange gain/loss).
Non-Monetary at Fair Value Revalued assets, investments carried at fair value. Translated at Exchange Rate on the Date Fair Value was Determined.
Non-Integral Foreign Operation (NIFO) Overseas branch/subsidiary operating with autonomy and independent cash flows. Exchange differences accumulated in Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR) in Equity.
Hedging Forward Contract Contract to hedge existing foreign currency monetary item. Premium/Discount amortized over contract life; exchange diff recognized in P&L.
Speculative Forward Contract Contract entered into for trading or speculation. No premium amortization; Marked to Market at balance sheet date with gain/loss in P&L.

2.5 AS 13 / Ind AS 109: Accounting for Investments

  • Current Investments: Readily realizable and intended to be held for ≤ 1 year. Valued at Lower of Cost and Fair Value (computed individually or by category).
  • Long-Term Investments: Carried at Historical Cost. Reduction in carrying amount made ONLY for permanent diminution (other than temporary decline).
  • Reclassification:
    • Current → Long-term: Transferred at lower of cost and fair value at date of transfer.
    • Long-term → Current: Transferred at lower of cost and carrying amount at date of transfer.
  • Pre-Acquisition Dividend: Dividends declared out of profits earned prior to acquisition are credited to the Investment Account to reduce acquisition cost (NOT recognized as income in P&L).
  • Bonus Shares Received: Increases quantity of shares; cost per share decreases; total carrying cost unchanged.
  • Rights Renunciation: Proceeds from sale of rights are credited to P&L as other income (unless shares were purchased cum-rights shortly before).
  • Ind AS 109 Classification: Financial assets classified into: (i) Amortised Cost, (ii) Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income (FVTOCI), or (iii) Fair Value through Profit or Loss (FVTPL) based on Business Model and SPPI test.

2.6 Depreciation Formulas Summary

SLM Annual Depreciation = (Cost - Residual Value) / Useful Life
WDV Depreciation = Book Value at Beginning of Year × Depreciation Rate
Depletion / Production Units Rate = (Cost - Salvage Value) / Total Estimated Units or Tonnes
Sum-of-the-Years'-Digits (SYD) = (Cost - Salvage Value) × [Remaining Useful Life / Σ Digits]

Cash Flow Statement (AS 3) & Fund Flow Statement

3.1 Three Activities in Cash Flow Statement (AS 3)

Activity Definition & Inclusions Exam Special Cases
Operating Activities Principal revenue-producing activities and other non-investing/financing activities. Cash from customers, payments to suppliers and employees. Interest/dividends paid & received are Operating for financial enterprises. Direct taxes paid are Operating unless specifically identifiable.
Investing Activities Acquisition and disposal of long-term assets and other investments not included in cash equivalents. Purchase/sale of PPE, intangibles, purchase/sale of shares/debentures of other firms. Dividend and interest received by non-financial entities is Investing. Insurance claims for building/plant damage are extraordinary Investing inflows.
Financing Activities Activities that alter the size and composition of owner's equity and borrowings. Issue of shares/debentures, bank loans, redemption of preference shares, buy-back. Dividend and interest paid by non-financial entities is Financing. Bank overdrafts repayable on demand forming part of cash management are Cash Equivalents.
EXAM TRAP: Non-Cash Transactions
Transactions like converting debentures into equity shares, acquiring machinery by issuing shares, or acquiring assets under finance lease involve NO cash movement and are EXCLUDED from the Cash Flow Statement (disclosed in notes).

3.2 Cash Equivalents Criteria

  • Short-term, highly liquid investments readily convertible into known amounts of cash.
  • Subject to an insignificant risk of changes in value.
  • Standard maturity threshold: 3 months or less (90/91 days) from the date of acquisition (e.g. 91-day Treasury Bills, Commercial Paper).
  • Movement between cash, bank balances, and cash equivalents is cash management, NOT cash inflow or outflow!

3.3 Funds Flow Statement: Concept & Rules

  • Definition of Funds: Net Working Capital = Current Assets (CA) - Current Liabilities (CL).
  • Golden Rule of Fund Flow: A transaction causes a flow of funds ONLY when it involves one Current Account and one Non-Current Account.
    • Current Account + Current Account = NO flow of funds (WC unchanged).
    • Non-Current Account + Non-Current Account = NO flow of funds (WC unchanged).
    • Current Account + Non-Current Account = FLOW OF FUNDS (Source or Application).
  • Schedule of Changes in Working Capital:
    • Increase in Current Asset → Increases Working Capital
    • Decrease in Current Asset → Decreases Working Capital
    • Increase in Current Liability → Decreases Working Capital
    • Decrease in Current Liability → Increases Working Capital
  • Funds from Operations (FFO) Formula:
    FFO = Net Profit + Non-Cash Charges (Depreciation, Goodwill amortized, Preliminary Exp written off) + Non-Operating Losses (Loss on sale of assets) - Non-Operating Incomes (Profit on sale of assets, Dividend received)

Financial Ratio Analysis & DuPont Decomposition

4.1 Master Formula & Interpretation Table

Ratio Name Standard Formula Benchmark / Ideal Core Significance
Current Ratio Current Assets / Current Liabilities 2.0 : 1 Measures short-term debt-paying ability.
Quick Ratio (Acid-Test) (Current Assets - Inventory - Prepaid Exp) / Current Liabilities 1.0 : 1 Strict instant liquidity test without relying on inventory sale.
Absolute Cash Ratio (Cash + Marketable Securities) / Current Liabilities 0.5 : 1 Measures immediate cash liquidity against payables.
Debt-Equity Ratio Total Long-Term Debt / Shareholders' Funds 2.0 : 1 (Sec 68) Capital structure leverage; high ratio = highly geared.
Proprietary Ratio Shareholders' Funds / Total Assets Higher is safer Proportion of assets financed by owners rather than creditors.
Capital Gearing Ratio Fixed-Cost Capital / Equity Shareholder Funds — (Preference Capital + Debentures) / (Equity Capital + Reserves).
Interest Coverage (ICR) EBIT / Annual Interest Expense > 3.0 times Margin of safety for interest servicing.
DSCR (EAT + Dep + Int + Non-Cash) / (Interest + Principal Repayment) 1.5 – 2.0 Critical bank project loan solvency test.
Inventory Turnover (ITR) COGS / Average Inventory Higher is faster Velocity of inventory conversion; Days = 365 / ITR.
Debtors Turnover (DTR) Net Credit Sales / Average Debtors — Speed of collection; Collection Period (DSO) = 365 / DTR.
Working Capital Turnover Net Sales / Net Working Capital — Efficiency of working capital utilization.
Operating Relationship Operating Ratio + Operating Profit Ratio = 100% Sum = 100% Operating Cost + Operating Profit = Net Sales.

4.2 DuPont Analysis Models

  • 3-Step DuPont Model:
    ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier
    ROE = (Net Profit / Sales) × (Sales / Total Assets) × (Total Assets / Shareholders' Equity)
    • Net Profit Margin: Operational efficiency & cost control.
    • Asset Turnover: Asset efficiency & revenue generation capacity.
    • Equity Multiplier: Financial leverage (Assets / Equity = 1 + Debt/Equity).
  • 5-Step DuPont Model:
    ROE = Tax Burden × Interest Burden × EBIT Margin × Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage
    ROE = (EAT / EBT) × (EBT / EBIT) × (EBIT / Sales) × (Sales / Assets) × (Assets / Equity)
  • Trading on Equity Rule: Financial leverage increases ROE if and only if Return on Capital Employed (ROCE/ROA) > Cost of Borrowed Debt.

4.3 Working Capital Cycle (Operating Cycle)

Net Operating Cycle = Raw Material Days + WIP Days + Finished Goods Days + Debtors Collection Days - Creditors Payment Days

Share Capital Transactions, Bonus/Rights, ESOPs & Buy-Back

5.1 Share Capital Statutory Rules (Companies Act, 2013)

  • Issue at Discount (Section 53): Strictly PROHIBITED; any share issued at a discount is void ab initio. Sole exception: Sweat Equity Shares under Section 54 (or debt restructuring under RBI schemes).
  • Sweat Equity (Section 54): Issued to directors/employees for intellectual property or value additions. Requires Special Resolution; valuation by Registered Valuer; mandatory 3-year lock-in period.
  • Calls in Arrears & Calls in Advance (Table F):
    • Calls in Arrears: Max interest chargeable is 10% p.a. (deducted from called-up capital).
    • Calls in Advance: Max interest payable is 12% p.a. (liability; paid before dividends).
  • Securities Premium Account Permissible Uses (Section 52(2)):
    1. Issuing fully paid bonus shares to members.
    2. Writing off preliminary expenses of the company.
    3. Writing off expenses/commission paid or discount allowed on issue of shares or debentures.
    4. Providing premium payable on redemption of redeemable preference shares or debentures (subject to Section 133 prescribed companies restrictions).
    5. Buy-back of own shares or other securities under Section 68.
  • Forfeiture & Reissue Rules:
    • If shares issued at premium are forfeited: (i) If premium already received → Securities Premium is NOT reversed; (ii) If premium unpaid → Securities Premium is debited.
    • Maximum Discount on Reissue: Cannot exceed the amount already forfeited (received) on those specific shares.
    • Capital Reserve on Reissue: Amount Forfeited on Reissued Shares - Reissue Discount Allowed = Transfer to Capital Reserve.

5.2 Bonus Shares (Section 63) vs. Rights Issue (Section 62)

Feature Bonus Shares (Section 63) Rights Issue (Section 62)
Permissible Sources Free Reserves, Securities Premium, Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR). Existing shareholders subscribing fresh capital at issue price.
Prohibited Sources Revaluation Reserve created by asset revaluation is strictly prohibited! —
Key Conditions Must be fully paid-up; no default in debt/deposits/statutory dues; cannot issue in lieu of dividend. Offer open for min 15 days to max 30 days. Right of renunciation exists unless articles say otherwise.
Key Formula Bonus Ratio: e.g. 1:2 bonus means 1 free share for every 2 shares held. Ex-Rights Price = [ (N × Cum Price) + Rights Price ] / (N + 1)
Value of Right = Cum Price - Ex-Rights Price = (Cum Price - Issue Price) / (N + 1)

5.3 Buy-Back of Securities (Sections 68, 69, 70)

  • Authorized Sources: (i) Free reserves, (ii) Securities premium account, (iii) Proceeds of fresh issue of any shares (but NOT out of fresh issue of the same kind of shares!).
  • Quantum Limits:
    • Board Resolution Limit: Up to 10% of total paid-up equity capital and free reserves.
    • Special Resolution Limit: Up to 25% of aggregate paid-up capital and free reserves in that FY.
    • Equity Share Volume Limit: Max 25% of total paid-up equity capital in any financial year.
  • Post-Buyback Debt-Equity Ratio: Must not exceed 2:1 (aggregate secured + unsecured debt ≤ 2 × [Paid-up capital + Free reserves]).
  • Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR): When bought back out of free reserves/securities premium, nominal face value of shares bought back must be transferred to CRR.
  • Time Limits:
    • Buy-back must be completed within 12 months of passing resolution.
    • Extinguishment & physical destruction of securities within 7 days of completion.
    • Cooling-off period: No further issue of same class of shares for 6 months (except bonus/ESOP).
    • No fresh buy-back offer within 1 year from closure of preceding buy-back.
  • Section 70 Prohibitions: Cannot buy back through subsidiaries, investment companies, or if defaulting on deposits, debentures, preference redemption, or term loans.

5.4 ESOPs (SEBI SBEB Regulations & Ind AS 102)

  • Vesting Period: Minimum 1 year between grant and vesting of options.
  • Accounting Valuation: Listed entities must use the Fair Value Method (Black-Scholes / Binomial models). Employee Compensation Expense is amortized on a straight-line basis over the vesting period.
  • Vested Options Lapsed: Accumulated balance in Employee Stock Options Outstanding is transferred to General Reserve within equity (cannot be credited back to P&L).
  • Cash-Settled (SARs): Measured at fair value of liability and remeasured at each balance sheet date with changes in P&L.

Company Final Accounts & Schedule III (Companies Act, 2013)

6.1 Balance Sheet Structure & Operating Cycle Rule

  • Equity & Liabilities: (1) Shareholders' Funds, (2) Share Application Money Pending Allotment, (3) Non-Current Liabilities, (4) Current Liabilities.
  • Assets: (1) Non-Current Assets, (2) Current Assets.
  • Operating Cycle: Time between acquisition of assets for processing and their realization in cash. If cannot be identified, statutory default is 12 months.
  • Current Maturities of Long-Term Debt: Presented under Other Current Liabilities (or Other Financial Liabilities under Ind AS Schedule III).
  • Proposed Dividend: Under revised AS 4, proposed dividend after balance sheet date is disclosed in Notes to Accounts (NOT recognized as a balance sheet liability).

6.2 Statement of Profit & Loss (Part II) Line Items

  • I. Revenue from Operations + II. Other Income = III. Total Income.
  • IV. Expenses Breakdown:
    • Cost of materials consumed: Opening Raw Material + Purchases - Closing Raw Material
    • Purchases of Stock-in-Trade
    • Changes in inventories of finished goods, WIP and stock-in-trade: Opening Inventories - Closing Inventories
    • Employee benefits expense (salaries, PF, gratuity, ESOP expense)
    • Finance costs (interest, borrowing costs, foreign exchange borrowing adjustments)
    • Depreciation and amortization expense
    • Other expenses (rent, rates, taxes, insurance, repairs, audit fees, legal charges)

6.3 Mandatory Schedule III Disclosures (MCA Amendments)

Mandatory Disclosure Regulatory Requirement & Format
Trade Receivables & Payables Ageing Bucketed into: < 1 yr, 1-2 yrs, 2-3 yrs, > 3 yrs. Separated between MSME vs Others, and Disputed vs Undisputed.
CWIP & Intangible Assets Under Dev Ageing schedule separating: (i) Projects in progress, and (ii) Projects temporarily suspended. Overdue timeline/cost details.
11 Mandatory Ratios & Variance Current Ratio, Debt-Equity, DSCR, ROE, Inventory Turnover, Trade Receivables Turnover, Trade Payables Turnover, Net Capital Turnover, Net Profit Margin, ROCE, Return on Investment. Mandatory explanation if variance > 25% compared to previous year!
Promoters' Shareholding Disclose promoter name, number of shares, % of total shares, and % change during the financial year.
Title Deeds of Immovable Property Disclose whether title deeds of immovable properties are held in the name of the company or promoters/relatives.
Special Governance Disclosures Wilful Defaulter declarations, Benami property proceedings, transactions with Struck-off companies, crypto/virtual currency trading, undisclosed income surrendered in tax assessments.
Downstream & Upstream Funding Disclosure of loans/investments given to Intermediaries to fund Ultimate Beneficiaries, with FEMA and PMLA compliance declarations.
CSR Disclosures (Section 135) Amount required to be spent, amount spent, shortfall/excess, reasons for shortfall, related party CSR transactions.
