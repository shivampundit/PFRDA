1. Marketing Management Orientations & Core Concepts

Marketing Philosophy Core Assumption / Starting Point Primary Focus & Risks
Production Concept Consumers favor products that are widely available and inexpensive. Focus on high production efficiency, low costs, mass distribution. Risk: Quality neglect.
Product Concept Consumers favor products offering the most quality, performance, innovation. Continuous engineering improvements. Major Risk: Marketing Myopia (Theodore Levitt, 1960).
Selling Concept Consumers won't buy enough without aggressive selling and promotion. Focus on closing transactions, hard selling. Common for unsought goods (insurance). High post-purchase dissonance.
Marketing Concept Delivering desired satisfaction more effectively than competitors. Customer-centric ('Sense and Respond'). Outside-in perspective. Customer satisfaction is the path to profit.
Holistic Marketing Kotler & Keller: Everything matters; integrated, broad perspective. Four Pillars: Relationship Marketing, Integrated Marketing, Internal Marketing, Performance/Socially Responsible Marketing.
📋 Foundational Marketing Frameworks & Mix Models
  • McCarthy's 4Ps (Product Mix): Product, Price, Place, Promotion
  • Booms & Bitner's 7Ps (Services Mix): 4Ps + People, Process, Physical Evidence
  • Lauterborn's 4Cs (Customer Orientation): Customer Solution, Customer Cost, Convenience, Communication
📐 Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) Valuation Model
CLV = M × [ r 1 + i − r ] − AC
M: Margin (Annual cash contribution per customer)
r: Retention Rate (Probability of repeat purchase)
i: Discount Rate (Cost of capital / Hurdle rate)
AC: Acquisition Cost per new customer

2. Marketing Research: Process, Designs, Scales & Sampling

Marketing Research Process: (1) Define Problem & Research Objectives → (2) Develop Research Plan → (3) Collect Data → (4) Analyze Information → (5) Present Findings → (6) Make Decision.

Research Design Objective Common Techniques
Exploratory Gain background information, clarify problems, formulate hypotheses. Literature search, Focus Groups (6-10 people), Depth Interviews, Projective techniques, Ethnography.
Descriptive Describe market characteristics, demographics, competitor shares. Surveys, Cross-sectional panels, Longitudinal studies, Observation, Scanner data.
Causal Test hypotheses about cause-and-effect relationships. Controlled Laboratory & Field Experiments, A/B Split Testing, Test Marketing.

Measurement Scales & Reliability

  • Nominal Scale: Strictly categorical labeling without ranking (e.g., Gender, Marital Status). Measure: Mode.
  • Ordinal Scale: Categorical with meaningful order/ranking, but unequal intervals (e.g., Preference ranking, Education levels). Measure: Median.
  • Interval Scale: Equal numerical distance between points, but arbitrary/no natural zero (e.g., Celsius/Fahrenheit temperature, 5-point Likert Scale). Measure: Mean, Std Dev.
  • Ratio Scale: Absolute, non-arbitrary true zero point allowing ratio comparisons (e.g., Sales revenue, Age, Weight, Market share). All statistical operations permitted.
  • Reliability vs. Validity: Reliability = Consistency/stability (measured via Cronbach's Alpha ≥ 0.70). Validity = Accuracy (measuring what it intends to measure: Content, Criterion, Construct validity).

3. Strategic Planning & Portfolio Analysis Models

Ansoff's Product-Market Growth Matrix:
• Existing Product + Existing Market = Market Penetration (lowest risk)
• Existing Product + New Market = Market Development (geographic/segment expansion)
• New Product + Existing Market = Product Development (R&D innovation)
• New Product + New Market = Diversification (highest risk; Related vs Unrelated/Conglomerate)
BCG Growth-Share Matrix Quadrant Market Growth Rate Relative Market Share Cash Flow & Prescribed Strategic Action
Stars High High Cash neutral or slight positive/negative. Invest heavily to maintain leadership.
Cash Cows Low High Substantial surplus cash generator. Harvest/milk to fund Stars and Question Marks.
Question Marks (Problem Child) High Low Heavy cash consumer. High uncertainty: decide whether to 'Build' or 'Divest'.
Dogs Low Low Cash traps. Liquidate, divest, or harvest minimally.

Porter's Frameworks

  • Porter's Five Forces: (1) Threat of New Entrants, (2) Bargaining Power of Buyers, (3) Bargaining Power of Suppliers, (4) Threat of Substitutes, (5) Rivalry Among Existing Competitors.
  • Generic Competitive Strategies: Cost Leadership, Differentiation, Focus (Cost Focus / Differentiation Focus). Danger: Being Stuck in the Middle.
  • Blue Ocean Strategy (Kim & Mauborgne): Create uncontested market space; ERRC Grid: Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create.

4. STP: Segmentation, Targeting & Positioning

Bases of Segmentation: Geographic (region, climate), Demographic (age, gender, income, lifecycle), Psychographic (AIO: Activities, Interests, Opinions; VALS: Values & Lifestyles), Behavioral (Occasion, Benefits sought, User status, Usage rate, Loyalty status).

Effective Segmentation Criteria (Kotler): Measurable, Substantial, Accessible, Differentiable, Actionable.

Targeting Strategy Description Key Advantage / Trade-off
Undifferentiated (Mass) Ignores segment differences; one single offer for whole market. Low production and marketing costs; high vulnerability to focused rivals.
Differentiated (Segmented) Targets multiple segments with customized offers for each. Higher total sales and customer satisfaction; substantially higher operating costs.
Concentrated (Niche) Pursues a large share of one or a few narrow market niches. High operating efficiency and customer loyalty; higher risk if niche declines.
Micromarketing Local marketing (cities/stores) or Individual (1-to-1 mass customization). Hyper-relevance; high logistical complexity and cost.
Positioning Dimensions (Keller):
• Points of Parity (POPs): Essential category table-stakes (Category POP) or associations designed to negate competitor advantages (Competitive POP).
• Points of Difference (PODs): Unique, highly valued attributes that consumers believe competitors cannot match.
• Positioning Errors: Underpositioning (vague), Overpositioning (too narrow), Confused positioning (contradictory claims), Doubtful positioning (unbelievable claims).

5. Product, Brand Management & Pricing Economics

Kotler's 5 Product Levels: (1) Core Benefit → (2) Basic Product → (3) Expected Product → (4) Augmented Product → (5) Potential Product.

New Product Development (Cooper's Stage-Gate): Idea Generation → Idea Screening → Concept Testing → Marketing Strategy → Business Analysis → Product Development → Test Marketing → Commercialization.

Brand Equity Models

  • Keller's CBBE Pyramid (Base to Apex): Salience (Identity) → Performance & Imagery (Meaning) → Judgments & Feelings (Response) → Resonance (Relationships / Loyalty).
  • Aaker's Brand Equity Model: Brand Loyalty, Brand Awareness, Perceived Quality, Brand Associations, Proprietary Brand Assets.
  • Kapferer's Brand Identity Prism: Physique, Personality, Culture, Relationship, Reflection, Self-Image.
  • Brand Architecture: Branded House (Masterbrand: Virgin, FedEx) vs House of Brands (Standalones: P&G, Unilever) vs Endorsed Brands (Courtyard by Marriott).

Pricing Formulas & Strategies

📐 Price Elasticity of Demand (PED) & Total Revenue Dynamics
PED = % Δ Quantity Demanded % Δ Price = ΔQ ΔP × P Q
|PED| > 1 (Elastic): Price ↑ → Total Revenue ↓ (Cut price to expand revenue)
|PED| < 1 (Inelastic): Price ↑ → Total Revenue ↑ (Pricing power exists)
|PED| = 1 (Unit Elastic): Total Revenue maximized at turning point
📊 Break-Even Volume Analysis (Cost-Volume-Profit)
Break-Even Quantity (QBE) = Total Fixed Costs (FC) Selling Price (P) − Variable Cost per unit (VC) = Fixed Costs Unit Contribution Margin
Contribution Margin Ratio: (P − VC) / P
Break-Even Sales (Value): Fixed Costs / Contribution Margin Ratio

6. Distribution Channels & Supply Chain Logistics

  • Channel Levels: Zero-Level (Direct D2C), One-Level (Manufacturer → Retailer → Consumer), Two-Level (Manufacturer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer).
  • Vertical Marketing Systems (VMS): Corporate VMS (single ownership across stages, e.g. Zara), Contractual VMS (franchises, cooperatives), Administered VMS (commanded by dominant market player, e.g. Walmart).
  • Distribution Intensity: Intensive (mass convenience goods), Selective (appliances, premium goods), Exclusive (luxury automobiles, designer fashion).
  • Logistics Formulas: Economic Order Quantity: EOQ = sqrt((2 * D * S) / H) (where D = annual demand, S = ordering cost, H = holding cost).
  • Bullwhip Effect: Slight retail demand fluctuations amplify into wild inventory swings upstream toward manufacturers. Mitigated via real-time EDI/POS sharing and Vendor Managed Inventory (VMI).

7. Promotion Mix, IMC & Digital Marketing Metrics

Promotional Tool Core Characteristics Key Application
Advertising Pervasive, impersonal, paid presentation by identified sponsor. 5 Ms: Mission, Money, Message, Media, Measurement. Build long-term brand equity, DAGMAR (ACCA), AIDA model.
Sales Promotion Short-term incentives ('buy now'). Consumer (coupons, discounts) vs Trade promotions (allowances, contests). Immediate sales spikes, inventory clearance.
Public Relations / Publicity High credibility, third-party unpaid editorial coverage, news stories. Corporate reputation, crisis management, launch buzz.
Personal Selling Two-way personal interaction. 7 Steps: Prospecting → Preapproach → Approach → Presentation → Objections → Closing → Follow-up. Complex, high-value B2B and consumer durable sales.
📐 Media Planning & Advertising Quantitative Metrics
GRP = Reach (%) × Average Frequency
CPM (Cost Per Mille) = [ Total Advertising Cost Total Impressions ] × 1,000
GRP: Total gross volume of audience exposure across schedule
TRP (Target Rating Point): GRP calculated strictly within defined demographic target
💻 Digital Performance & Search Advertising Formulas
CTR (%) = Total Clicks Total Impressions × 100 | CPA = Total Ad Spend Total Conversions
ROAS = Gross Revenue from Ads Total Advertising Cost | Ad Rank = Max CPC Bid × Quality Score

9. Soft Skills, Professional Communication & Emotional Intelligence

  • 7 Cs of Communication: Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous.
  • Communication Process: Sender → Encoding → Message (Channel + Noise) → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback. Models: Shannon-Weaver (Noise), Berlo's SMCR (Source, Message, Channel, Receiver).
  • Active Listening: 3 As = Attitude, Attention, Adjustment. 4 Levels = Discriminative, Comprehensive, Critical/Evaluative, Empathetic. Tools: Paraphrasing, Reflective summarizing.
  • Body Language (Non-Verbal): Kinesics (gestures, postures, facial micro-expressions - Paul Ekman), Proxemics (Edward T. Hall's personal space zones: Intimate 0-1.5 ft, Personal 1.5-4 ft, Social 4-12 ft, Public 12+ ft), Vocalics/Paralanguage (pitch, pace, tone, pauses).
  • Clarity in Writing: Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle (Top-Down executive communication, SCQA framework, MECE structure: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive).
  • Conflict Resolution (Thomas-Kilmann TKI): 2 Dimensions: Assertiveness vs Cooperativeness. 5 Modes: Competing (win-lose), Collaborating (win-win), Compromising (middle-ground), Avoiding (no-win), Accommodating (lose-win).
  • Principled Negotiation (Fisher & Ury): Focus on interests, not positions; Separate people from problem; Invent options for mutual gain; Objective criteria; BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement); ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement).
  • Public Speaking (Aristotle's Rhetoric): Ethos (credibility), Pathos (emotional resonance), Logos (logic/reasoning). Rule of Three. Managing Glossophobia.
  • Emotional Intelligence (Daniel Goleman): 5 Domains: Self-Awareness, Self-Regulation, Motivation, Empathy, Social Skills. Johari Window: Open Arena, Blind Spot (reduced by feedback), Hidden Façade (reduced by disclosure), Unknown. Amygdala Hijack vs Prefrontal Cortex.
