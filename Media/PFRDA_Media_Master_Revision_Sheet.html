1. Micro-Targeting & Digital Advertising Economics

1.1 Micro-Targeting & Psychographics

  • Definition: Data-driven advertising utilizing individual digital footprints, browsing telemetry, and psychographic profiles to deliver hyper-personalized messaging to narrow micro-cohorts.
  • OCEAN Model (Big Five): Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism. Famously weaponized by Cambridge Analytica (2018) to tailor political messaging to psychological vulnerabilities.
  • Programmatic Advertising Ecosystem:
    • Demand-Side Platform (DSP): Software used by advertisers to automate real-time ad buying across multiple exchanges.
    • Supply-Side Platform (SSP): Software used by digital publishers to sell, manage, and optimize ad inventory.
    • Ad Exchange & RTB: Real-Time Bidding auction clearing bids within ~100 milliseconds during page load.
    • DMP vs. CDP: Data Management Platforms aggregate anonymous 3rd-party cookie segments temporarily; Customer Data Platforms unify 1st-party PII into a persistent single customer view.
  • Third-Party Cookie Deprecation: Browsers phasing out cross-site tracking cookies, driving the shift toward first-party data and Google's Privacy Sandbox (Topics API).
  • Apple ATT (App Tracking Transparency): iOS opt-in framework severely limiting mobile ad tracking via IDFA.
  • DPDP Act 2023 (Section 9(3)): Strictly prohibits behavioral tracking, monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children (<18 years).

2. Consumer Behaviour in Digital Media

2.1 Models of Consumer Decision Making & Adoption

Framework Author & Year Core Stages / Concepts
AIDA Model E. St. Elmo Lewis (1898) Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Hierarchical journey of consumer response.
Diffusion of Innovations Everett Rogers (1962) Innovators (2.5%), Early Adopters (13.5%), Early Majority (34%), Late Majority (34%), Laggards (16%).
Crossing the Chasm Geoffrey Moore (1991) The perilous chasm lies between Early Adopters (visionaries) and Early Majority (pragmatists).
Uses & Gratifications (UGT) Blumler & Katz (1974) Audiences are active agents seeking media to fulfill cognitive, affective, personal integrative, social, or escapist needs.

2.2 Digital Economics Metrics & Behavioral Biases

  • Attention Economy (Herbert Simon, 1971): A wealth of information creates a poverty of attention; human attention is the primary scarce resource.
  • Paradox of Choice (Barry Schwartz): Excessive catalog options cause decision paralysis, anxiety, and disengagement on streaming apps.
  • Dark Patterns (CCPA Guidelines 2023): Deceptive UI tricks (Roach Motel, Confirmshaming, Forced Continuity, Drip Pricing) prohibited under Indian consumer law.
  • Churn Rate Formula:
    📐 OTT Subscriber Churn Rate Model
    Churn Rate (%) = Subscribers Lost During Period Subscribers at Beginning of Period × 100
  • LTV to CAC Ratio:
    📊 Customer Economics & Capital Efficiency Ratio
    Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) ≥ 3.0
    LTV/CAC < 1.0: Value destroying (Destroys capital on each user)
    LTV/CAC = 3.0: Optimal healthy industry benchmark
    LTV/CAC > 5.0: Under-investing in user acquisition growth

3. Media Convergence & Extended Reality (VR/AR/XR)

3.1 Henry Jenkins' Convergence Culture (2006)

  • Four Dimensions: Technological Convergence (devices), Economic Convergence (conglomerate mergers), Cultural Convergence (participatory audience), Organic Convergence (multitasking).
  • Black Box Fallacy: The mistaken belief that one universal hardware device will replace all others. In reality, hardware proliferates while content converges across screens.
  • Transmedia Storytelling: A narrative unfolded across multiple platforms where each medium makes a distinctive, non-redundant contribution (e.g. The Matrix). Distinct from cross-platform syndication (which merely duplicates content).
  • Net Neutrality in India (TRAI, 2016): Prohibition of Discriminatory Tariffs for Data Services Regulations banned zero-rating and content-based pricing discrimination (banning Facebook Free Basics).

3.2 Extended Reality (XR) Taxonomy

Technology Environmental Immersion Physical World Visibility Representative Hardware / Examples
Virtual Reality (VR) 100% digital immersion; complete occlusion of physical reality. Zero (opaque display). Meta Quest 3, HTC Vive, PlayStation VR.
Augmented Reality (AR) Digital graphics/data superimposed over physical world. Physical world fully visible. Pokémon Go, Google Lens, heads-up automotive displays.
Mixed Reality (MR) Physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time with occlusion. Physical world viewed via optical see-through or video pass-through. Apple Vision Pro, Microsoft HoloLens 2.

3.3 Key Technical Parameters in XR

  • 3DoF vs. 6DoF: 3DoF tracks rotational orientation only (Pitch, Yaw, Roll); 6DoF tracks rotational orientation PLUS 3 translational physical movements (Surging, Heaving, Swaying in 3D space).
  • Motion-to-Photon Latency: Must remain strictly < 20 milliseconds to prevent visual-vestibular mismatch and cybersickness.
  • Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex (VOR): Human reflex rotating eyes opposite to head motion; visual latency must match VOR to avoid nausea.
  • Immersion vs. Presence (Mel Slater): Immersion is objective system technology (FoV, resolution); Presence is the subjective psychological feeling of 'being there'.
  • Foveated Rendering: Eye-tracking renders only the central foveal vision in 4K while lowering peripheral resolution, cutting GPU load by 50–75%.

4. Digital Divide & Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)

4.1 Jan van Dijk's Three Levels of Digital Divide

Level Core Barrier Intervention Strategy
First-Level Divide Inequality in physical access to hardware, devices, electricity, and broadband networks. BharatNet (optical fiber to 2.5 lakh Gram Panchayats), PM-WANI (public Wi-Fi PDOs).
Second-Level Divide Inequality in digital skills, operational literacy, information evaluation, and navigation. PMGDISHA (Pradhan Mantri Gramin Digital Saksharta Abhiyan), digital literacy programs.
Third-Level Divide Inequality in tangible real-world outcomes and social/economic benefits derived from digital participation. Digital Public Infrastructure (India Stack, UPI, ONDC, DigiLocker, Account Aggregator).

4.2 Key Indian Initiatives & Accessibility

  • Gender Digital Divide (NFHS-5): Only ~33% of rural women in India have used the internet, compared to over 54% of urban women.
  • Universal Service Obligation Fund (USOF): 5% AGR levy on telecom operators funding rural connectivity (reformed as *Digital Bharat Nidhi* under Telecommunications Act, 2023).
  • Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.1/2.2): Principles of POUR (Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust) ensuring digital accessibility for persons with disabilities.

5. Audience Reception Theory & Cultural Representation

5.1 Classic Media Effect Theories

Theory Proponent(s) & Year Core Theoretical Principle
Hypodermic Needle Harold Lasswell (1927) Media shoots messages directly into passive audience minds with uniform, powerful effects.
Two-Step Flow Lazarsfeld & Katz (1944/55) Media → Opinion Leaders → Less active public via interpersonal networks.
Cultivation Theory George Gerbner (1976) Heavy TV viewing cultivates the 'Mean World Syndrome', mainstreaming, and resonance.
Agenda Setting McCombs & Shaw (1972) Media tells us 'what to think about' (1st level) and 'how to think about it' (2nd level framing).
Spiral of Silence Noelle-Neumann (1974) Fear of isolation causes perceived minority opinion holders to remain silent.
Third-Person Effect W. Phillips Davison (1983) People believe media influences *others* far more than *themselves*.

5.2 Stuart Hall's Encoding / Decoding Model (1973)

Decoding Position Audience Interpretation Behavior
Dominant-Hegemonic Viewer decodes the message directly in terms of the reference code in which it was produced; uncritically accepts the dominant ideology.
Negotiated Viewer acknowledges the broad legitimacy of the hegemonic message, but adapts the rules with local, personal exceptions.
Oppositional Viewer understands both literal and connotative meanings, but completely deconstructs and rejects the message within an alternative ideological framework.

5.3 Cultural Media Concepts

  • Symbolic Annihilation (Tuchman & Gerbner): Omission, trivialization, or ridicule of marginalized groups in media.
  • The Male Gaze (Laura Mulvey, 1975): Cinema structured visually around a heterosexual male spectator's scopophilic desires.
  • Cultural Hegemony (Antonio Gramsci): Ruling class maintains power by manufacturing cultural consent through civil institutions.
  • Orientalism (Edward Said, 1978): Western patronizing, exoticized representation of the East as backward and irrational.
  • Glocalization (Roland Robertson): Interplay of global formats with local cultural adaptations (e.g. KBC).
  • Soft Power (Joseph Nye, 1990): Ability to attract and co-opt rather than coerce through culture, cinema, and values.

6. OTT Platforms, Streaming Economics & Social Algorithms

6.1 IT Rules, 2021 Three-Tier Grievance Redressal

  • Level I (Publisher): Appoint an India-resident Grievance Redressal Officer; acknowledge in 24 hours, resolve in 15 days.
  • Level II (Self-Regulating Bodies): Headed by a retired Supreme Court / High Court judge, registered with MIB.
  • Level III (Government Oversight): Inter-Departmental Committee (IDC) chaired by Joint Secretary, MIB.
  • Age Ratings: U, U/A 7+, U/A 13+, U/A 16+, A. Mandatory parental locks for U/A 13+ and above.

6.2 Recommendation Engine Architecture

  • Two-Tower Model: User Tower (user features/history → vector u) + Item Tower (item metadata → vector v). Fast candidate retrieval via dot product $\langle u, v angle$.
  • Collaborative Filtering: Matrix Factorization (SVD) decomposing interaction matrix $R pprox P imes Q^T$. Suffers from the *Cold-Start Problem*.
  • Content-Based Filtering: Recommends items similar in genre/metadata to past user watches.
  • Hooked Model (Nir Eyal): Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment. Explains infinite scroll and pull-to-refresh addiction.
  • Virality Metric:
    🚀 Digital Virality Coefficient (K-Factor)
    K-factor = i × c
    i: Average number of invitations sent per active user
    c: Average conversion rate per invitation received
    K > 1.0: Exponential self-sustaining organic viral growth

7. Market Trends, Global Governance & Executive Negotiation Skills

7.1 Market Trends (2024–2026)

  • Creator Economy & 1,000 True Fans (Kevin Kelly): Shift from volatile ad CPMs to direct fan monetization (Patreon, Substack).
  • Influencer Regulations (CCPA 2019 & ASCI 2023): Fines up to ₹50 Lakh on endorsers for misleading ads; financial influencers (Finfluencers) must hold SEBI registration or finance qualifications.
  • Generative AI & Copyright: Human authorship requirement under Indian Copyright Act (Section 2(d)(vi)) and US Copyright Office.
  • Global Governance: EU Digital Services Act (DSA - VLOP audits) & Digital Markets Act (DMA - Gatekeepers); Australian News Media Bargaining Code (2021).

7.2 Negotiation Master Guide (Harvard Model)

Concept Definition & Strategic Value
BATNA Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement (Fisher & Ury). Primary source of bargaining power; determines walk-away threshold.
ZOPA Zone of Possible Agreement. Positive overlap between Buyer's Max Reservation Price and Seller's Min Reservation Price.
Reservation Price The walk-away point below/above which you will pursue your BATNA instead.
Distributive vs. Integrative Distributive: Zero-sum, fixed-pie single issue. Integrative: Win-Win, expanding the pie via trade-offs on asymmetric priorities (*Logrolling*).
Harvard 4 Principles (1) Separate people from problem; (2) Focus on interests, not positions; (3) Invent options for mutual gain; (4) Insist on objective criteria.
Thomas-Kilmann Modes (TKI) Competing (win-lose), Collaborating (win-win), Compromising (split difference), Avoiding (withdraw), Accommodating (yield).
