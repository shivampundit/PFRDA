🎯 2. Quadratic Equations: The 15-Second Sign Shortcut

For standard regulatory body quadratic equations ax² + bx + c = 0, roots can be determined rapidly by inspecting signs:

Equation Form Sign of b Sign of c Root 1 Sign Root 2 Sign Direct Comparison Rule
x² - bx + c = 0-+++Both roots are always POSITIVE.
x² + bx + c = 0++--Both roots are always NEGATIVE.
x² - bx - c = 0--+-Larger root is (+), Smaller root is (-).
x² + bx - c = 0+--+Larger root is (-), Smaller root is (+).
Zero-Calculation Exam Shortcuts:
  1. If Equation I is x² + bx + c = 0 (roots -,-) and Equation II is y² - by + c = 0 (roots +,+), then without finding roots: x < y.
  2. If the constant term c is NEGATIVE in both equations (c < 0), roots are (+,-) and (+,-). Therefore, Relationship Cannot Be Established (CND) holds true 100% of the time!

🔢 3. Number Series: 8 Recurring Regulatory Patterns

1. Differences of Squares / Cubes

Consecutive differences are n² or n³ (e.g., +9, +25, +49, +81, +121 or +8, +27, +64, +125).

2. Prime Number Gaps

Differences are consecutive prime numbers (e.g., +17, +19, +23, +29, +31, +37).

3. Multiplicative + Additive Series

n × 1 + 1, n × 2 + 2, n × 3 + 3... or n × 2 + 1, n × 3 + 2, n × 4 + 3...

4. Half-Multiplier (0.5 Step) Series

×0.5 + 1, ×1 + 1.5, ×1.5 + 2, ×2 + 2.5... (Notice when early terms hover near the initial value).

5. Alternating Operations

+1², -2², +3², -4²... or +k, -m, +k, -m... (Seen in SEBI 2025 wrong number series: 600, 604, 577, 593...).

6. Sum of Digits Power Pattern

Next term = Previous term + (Sum of digits)² (Seen in PFRDA recollected papers).

📐 4. High-Yield Arithmetic Master Formula Sheet

Percentage & Profit/Loss

• Net Successive % Change = a + b + (ab / 100)% • Selling Price (SP) = Marked Price (MP) × (100 - Discount%) / 100 • MP / CP Ratio = (100 + Profit%) / (100 - Discount%) • Margin of Safety = Actual Sales - Break-Even Sales = Profit / (P/V Ratio)

Simple & Compound Interest

• Simple Interest (SI) = (P × R × T) / 100 • Compound Interest (CI) = P × [ (1 + R/100)^T - 1 ] • 2-Year Difference (CI - SI) = P × (R / 100)² • 3-Year Difference (CI - SI) = P × (R / 100)² × [ 3 + (R / 100) ] • Rule of 72: Doubling Period = 72 / Interest Rate (years)

Partnerships & Active Partner Calculations

• Profit Sharing Ratio = (Capital_A × Time_A) : (Capital_B × Time_B) : (Capital_C × Time_C) • Working Partner Salary: Deduct salary from Total Profit FIRST, then distribute remaining balance according to investment ratio.

Mixtures & Repeated Dilution

• Alligation Rule: (Cheaper Qty / Dearer Qty) = (Price Dearer - Mean Price) / (Mean Price - Price Cheaper) • Repeated Replacement Formula: Remaining Pure Liquid = Initial_Qty × (1 - x / V)^n where V = total capacity, x = quantity drawn out each time, n = number of operations.

Time & Work, Pipes & Cisterns

• Unit Work Approach: Total Work = LCM of individual completion days. Efficiency = Total Units / Days. • Net Filling Rate = Inlet Efficiency - Leak / Drain Efficiency • Alternate Days Work: Calculate output for 1 full cycle, multiply cycles to approach total, solve remainder sequentially.

Time, Speed, Distance, Trains & Boats

• Unit Conversion: 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s | 1 m/s = 18/5 km/h • Average Speed (equal distance at speeds u and v) = 2uv / (u + v) • Relative Speed: Same Direction = |s₁ - s₂| | Opposite Direction = s₁ + s₂ • Train crossing Platform = (Length_Train + Length_Platform) / Speed • Downstream Speed (D) = u + v | Upstream Speed (U) = u - v • Boat Speed in Still Water (u) = (D + U) / 2 • Stream / Current Speed (v) = (D - U) / 2

⚖️ 6. Coded & Linear Inequalities: Open Gate Rule

The "Open Gate" Visual Strategy:
  • A > B: Gate is open from left (A) to right (B). You can travel from A to B, but never backwards.
  • A ≥ B: Gate open from A to B. For A ≥ B to be true across a multi-variable chain, EVERY single gate along the path must have an equal sign (≥ or =). If even one strict gate (>) appears, the conclusion reduces to A > B!
  • Conflicting Symbols (> and <): If you encounter opposing gate directions (e.g., A > B < C), the chain is blocked. No definite conclusion exists between A and C.
  • Either-Or Condition: Both conclusions must be individually false, the elements must be identical, and combining them must cover all possibilities (>, <, =).

🏢 7. Puzzles & Seating: 4-Phase Solving Algorithm

Phase 1: Scanning & Triage

Spend 20 seconds reading variable count. Prioritize single-variable linear rows or 8-floor structures over dual-variable month-date puzzles.

Phase 2: Anchor Identification

Identify fixed anchors: "X sits at an extreme end", "Y lives on Floor 8", or "Z sits third to the right of corner person".

Phase 3: Two-Case Parallel Tracing

Immediately draw Case 1 and Case 2 side-by-side. Never solve on a single mental branch. Place definite clues on both grids simultaneously.

Phase 4: Negative Clue Elimination

Apply restrictive negative clues ("Neither A nor B likes Blue", "C does not sit near D") to eliminate one case cleanly, leaving the unique final arrangement.

🧠 8. Critical Reasoning Master Rules

The Assumption Negation Test:

An assumption is an unstated premise without which the author's argument falls apart. Negate the option: if negating it makes the conclusion completely illogical or impossible, that option is an implicit assumption!

Strengthening vs Weakening Arguments:

• To Strengthen: Introduce evidence supporting the unstated assumption, or rule out an alternate cause.
• To Weaken: Show that the cause does not lead to the effect, or introduce a compelling alternative explanation.

📖 9. 40 Golden Grammar Rules for Error Detection

Rule 1: Along with / As well as

Verb agrees with the FIRST subject when joined by along with, as well as, together with, in addition to. (e.g. The chairman, along with his directors, is attending.)

Rule 2: Neither... Nor / Either... Or

Verb agrees with the NEAREST subject. (e.g. Neither the CEO nor the managers appear willing.)

Rule 3: Inversion after Negative Adverbs

Words like Hardly, Scarcely, Seldom, Barely, No sooner placed at the beginning require subject-auxiliary inversion. (e.g. Hardly had the circular been issued...)

Rule 4: Correlative Conjunction Pairs

• Hardly / Scarcely + when (never 'than').
• No sooner + than (never 'when').
• Not only + but also.
• Although + yet (or comma, never 'but').

Rule 5: Uncountable Nouns

Words like information, furniture, luggage, equipment, advice, machinery, jewellery never take plural 's'. Say pieces of information, never informations.

Rule 6: Absolute Adjectives

Words like unique, perfect, supreme, eternal, ideal, impossible cannot take degrees of comparison. Say unique, not very unique or more perfect.

Rule 7: Preposition + Gerund

A verb immediately following a preposition must be in the gerund (V+ing) form (e.g. for reducing inflation, not for reduce).

Rule 8: Redundancy Traps

Never say discussed about, revert back, return back, or supposing if.

Rule 9: Between vs Among

Between is for two distinct entities (or mutual treaties); Among is for groups of three or more.

Rule 10: One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb

One of the officers was selected. BUT if followed by a relative pronoun: He is one of the officers who were selected.
