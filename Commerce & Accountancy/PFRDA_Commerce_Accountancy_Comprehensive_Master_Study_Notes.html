1. Accounting as a Financial Information System & Theoretical Framework

Accounting is a formal measurement, communication, and decision-support discipline that identifies, records, classifies, summarizes, and interprets economic events to enable informed decision-making by diverse internal and external stakeholders.

Foundational Accounting Concepts & Conventions

Concept / Convention Core Regulatory & Conceptual Principle High-Yield Exam Trap & Application
Going Concern Enterprise will continue operational existence for the foreseeable future (no intent to liquidate). Fixed assets recorded at historical cost less depreciation, NOT at current net realisable liquidation value.
Accrual Concept Revenues and expenses recognized as earned or incurred, regardless of cash receipt or payment. Underlies all period-end adjustments: Outstanding Expenses, Prepaid Expenses, Accrued Incomes.
Conservatism / Prudence Anticipate all prospective losses, but anticipate zero prospective or unrealized gains. Inventory valued at Cost or NRV, whichever is lower; Provision for doubtful debts created; contingent assets ignored.
Materiality Financial statements disclose all items significant enough to influence economic decisions. Small expense items (e.g. ₹500 waste basket) expensed immediately rather than capitalized over multi-year useful life.
Money Measurement Only transactions capable of expression in monetary terms are recorded in financial accounting. Human capital, employee morale, brand reputation cannot appear on historical balance sheet.

2. Accounting Standards Master Guide (AS vs. Ind AS vs. IFRS)

Accounting Standards standardize financial reporting across corporate India. Under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) notifies Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS), converged with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS).

Core Prescribed Standards Master Comparison

Subject Area Indian AS (Traditional) Converged Ind AS Core Measurement Principles & Differences
Inventories AS 2 Ind AS 2 • Valued at Lower of Cost and Net Realizable Value (NRV).
• Cost formulas permitted: FIFO or Weighted Average Cost (WAC).
• LIFO is strictly prohibited under both AS 2 and Ind AS 2!
Cash Flow Statement AS 3 Ind AS 7 • Classifies into Operating, Investing, and Financing Activities.
• Cash and Cash Equivalents = High liquidity investments readily convertible with insignificant risk of change in value (maturity ≤ 3 months).
• Bank overdrafts repayable on demand are part of Cash & Cash Equivalents under Ind AS 7.
Depreciation & Fixed Assets AS 10 / AS 6 (Withdrawn) Ind AS 16 (PPE) • PPE recognized if future economic benefits probable and cost reliably measurable.
• Depreciation is systematic allocation of depreciable amount over useful life.
• Component Depreciation mandatory under Ind AS 16 and Schedule II of Companies Act.
• Revaluation Model vs Cost Model options.
Revenue Recognition AS 9 Ind AS 115 • AS 9 relies on transfer of significant risks and rewards of ownership.
• Ind AS 115 (5-Step Model): 1. Identify Contract → 2. Identify Performance Obligations → 3. Determine Transaction Price → 4. Allocate Price → 5. Recognize Revenue upon satisfaction of Performance Obligations.
Foreign Exchange AS 11 Ind AS 21 • Monetary items reported using closing rate; non-monetary at historical rate.
• Functional currency concept established under Ind AS 21.
• Exchange differences recognized in P&L (except qualifying long-term monetary assets/liabilities under FCMITDA).
Investments AS 13 Ind AS 109 / Ind AS 32 • AS 13: Current Investments at Lower of Cost and Fair Value; Long-term at Cost less permanent diminution.
• Ind AS 109: Classified into: Amortized Cost, FVTOCI (Fair Value through OCI), and FVTPL (Fair Value through P&L) based on business model test and SPPI test.
📊 Frequently Tested Concept: Ind AS 115 5-Step Model

1. Identify the contract with the customer.
2. Identify the separate performance obligations in the contract.
3. Determine the transaction price (accounting for variable consideration, financing component).
4. Allocate the transaction price to performance obligations based on relative standalone selling prices.
5. Recognize revenue when (or as) the entity satisfies a performance obligation (over time vs at a point in time).

3. Cash Flow Statement (AS 3 / Ind AS 7), Funds Flow & Financial Ratio Analysis

Cash Flow Statement Mechanics: Operating, Investing & Financing

⚠ Treatment of Interest & Dividend (Non-Financial Company vs Financial Company)

Non-Financial Company:
• Dividend Paid: Always Financing Activity.
• Interest Paid: Always Financing Activity.
• Dividend Received: Always Investing Activity.
• Interest Received: Always Investing Activity.
Financial Company (Banks / NBFCs): Interest paid, interest received, and dividend received are treated as Operating Activities. Dividend paid remains Financing Activity.

Comprehensive Ratio Analysis & DuPont Framework

1. Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities (Benchmark 2:1)
2. Quick Ratio (Acid Test) = (Current Assets - Inventory - Prepaid Expenses) / Current Liabilities (Benchmark 1:1)
3. Debt to Equity Ratio = Total Long-Term Debt / Shareholders' Equity (Benchmark 2:1)
4. Interest Coverage Ratio = EBIT / Interest Expense
5. Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) / Average Inventory
6. Debtors Turnover Ratio = Net Credit Sales / Average Accounts Receivable
7. DuPont Return on Equity (ROE 3-Step Decomposition):
    ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier
    ROE = (Net Profit / Sales) × (Sales / Total Assets) × (Total Assets / Equity)

Funds Flow Statement vs Cash Flow Statement

Parameter Funds Flow Statement Cash Flow Statement
Concept of Funds Broader concept: Net Working Capital (Current Assets − Current Liabilities). Narrower concept: Cash & Cash Equivalents (Liquid cash and short-term paper ≤ 3 months).
Accounting Basis Accrual basis of accounting. Strict cash basis of accounting.
Schedule Required Mandatory Statement of Changes in Working Capital. No separate schedule; changes absorbed into operating cash flow.
Statutory Requirement Not mandatory under Companies Act, 2013; used for internal management analysis. Statutory mandatory component of financial statements under Section 2(40) (except OPC, Small Co, Dormant Co).

4. Share Capital Transactions, Forfeiture, Bonus, Rights, ESOPs & Buy-Back

Share Forfeiture & Reissue Mechanics

When a shareholder defaults on allotment or call money, the Board of Directors can forfeit the shares after serving a 14-day notice under Articles of Association.

At Forfeiture:
Share Capital A/c (Called-up Amount) ................. Dr.
    To Share Allotment / Call A/c (Unpaid Amount)
    To Share Forfeiture A/c (Actual Amount Paid by Shareholder)

At Reissue at Discount:
Bank A/c (Actual Cash Received) ....................... Dr.
Share Forfeiture A/c (Discount on Reissue) ............ Dr.
    To Share Capital A/c (Paid-up Amount)

Transfer of Balance to Capital Reserve:
Share Forfeiture A/c ................................. Dr.
    To Capital Reserve A/c (Net Profit on Reissued Shares)
📊 Crucial Statutory Law on Reissue Discount

Under the Companies Act, 2013, the discount allowed on reissue of forfeited shares cannot exceed the amount already collected on those specific shares credited to the Share Forfeiture Account. The net gain on reissue of forfeited shares is a capital gain and must be transferred to Capital Reserve Account.

Statutory Rules for Corporate Actions under Companies Act, 2013

Corporate Action Section of Co. Act, 2013 Permitted Sources & Key Statutory Restrictions
Bonus Shares Section 63 • Permitted sources: Free Reserves, Securities Premium, Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR).
• Cannot be issued out of Revaluation Reserve created by revaluation of assets.
• Cannot be issued in lieu of dividend.
• Company must not have defaulted on debt/deposits/statutory dues.
Right Shares Section 62(1)(a) • Pre-emptive rights offered to existing equity shareholders in proportion to paid-up share capital.
• Offer period: 15 to 30 days.
• Shareholders have statutory right to renounce unless Articles state otherwise.
ESOPs Section 62(1)(b) • Special resolution approval required.
• Minimum vesting period: 1 year.
• Ineligible: Promoters, directors holding >10% shares (except startup companies for 10 years).
Buy-Back of Securities Sections 68, 69, 70 • Permitted sources: Free reserves, Securities Premium, proceeds of earlier issue of different class.
• Board Approval Limit: Up to 10% of paid-up capital + free reserves.
• Special Resolution Limit: Up to 25% of paid-up capital + free reserves in a financial year.
• Post Buy-Back Debt-Equity Ratio ≤ 2:1.
• Creation of Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR) equal to nominal value of shares bought back out of free reserves.

Operating Cycle Rule & Current vs Non-Current Distinction

  • Operating Cycle: The time between the acquisition of assets for processing and their realization in cash or cash equivalents. When the normal operating cycle cannot be identified, it is assumed to have a duration of 12 months.
  • Current Asset: Expected to be realized, sold, or consumed in normal operating cycle, or held primarily for trading, or realized within 12 months after reporting date, or cash/cash equivalent without restrictions.
  • Current Liability: Settled in normal operating cycle, held primarily for trading, due to be settled within 12 months, or company has no unconditional right to defer settlement for ≥ 12 months.

6. Deep-Dive Numerical Worked Examples & SEBI/PFRDA Exam Traps (2026 Edition)

6.1 Ind AS 115 (5-Step Revenue Recognition Model) vs. AS 9

Under Ind AS 115 (converged with IFRS 15), revenue is recognized when control of promised goods or services is transferred to the customer through the mandatory 5-Step Framework: (1) Identify the contract with a customer; (2) Identify distinct performance obligations; (3) Determine the transaction price (adjusting for variable consideration, time value of money, and non-cash consideration); (4) Allocate the transaction price to performance obligations based on Standalone Selling Prices (SSP); and (5) Recognize revenue when (or as) each performance obligation is satisfied (over time or at a point in time). By contrast, legacy AS 9 relies on the transfer of significant risks and rewards of ownership (completed service or proportionate completion method).

6.2 Worked Numerical: Share Forfeiture, Re-issue & Capital Reserve Transfer

Worked Example (PFRDA/SEBI Pattern): Alpha Ltd. forfeited 1,000 equity shares of ₹10 each (issued at a 20% premium of ₹2) for non-payment of allotment money of ₹5 (including ₹2 premium) and final call of ₹3. The application money of ₹4 per share had been duly received. Out of these, 600 shares were re-issued to Ramesh as fully paid-up for ₹8 per share.
• Step 1 (Amount Forfeited per Share): Since Securities Premium (₹2) was not received, Section 52 permits debiting Securities Premium A/c on forfeiture. Amount actually received towards share capital = ₹4 per share.
• Step 2 (Re-issue Discount per Share): Paid-up value ₹10 − Re-issue price ₹8 = ₹2 discount per share (debited to Share Forfeiture A/c).
• Step 3 (Transfer to Capital Reserve on 600 Re-issued Shares): 600 × (₹4 forfeited per share − ₹2 discount used per share) = ₹1,200 transferred to Capital Reserve! The balance of 400 × ₹4 = ₹1,600 remains in the Share Forfeiture Account until those 400 shares are re-issued.

6.3 Worked Numerical: Buy-Back of Shares (Section 68 Resource, Shares & Debt-Equity Tests)

When computing the Maximum Permissible Buy-Back under Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013, a company must take the lowest of three statutory limits: (1) Shares Outstanding Test: Maximum 25% of existing paid-up equity shares in a financial year; (2) Resource Test: Maximum 25% of total paid-up capital and free reserves (including Securities Premium Account for Section 68 purposes); and (3) Debt-Equity Ratio Test: Post-buy-back ratio of secured and unsecured debt owed by the company must not exceed 2:1 of post-buy-back paid-up capital and free reserves (keeping in mind that an amount equal to the nominal value of shares bought back out of free reserves/securities premium must also be transferred to the Capital Redemption Reserve [CRR] under Section 69, reducing free reserves doubly!).

Concept / Convention Core Theoretical Meaning Exam Practical Application
Business Entity Business is distinct and separate from its owners/proprietors. Proprietor's capital is a liability; owner's personal expenses debited to Drawings Account.
Money Measurement Only transactions capable of being expressed in monetary terms are recorded. Employee morale, management skill, brand loyalty are excluded from balance sheets.
Periodicity (Time Period) Indefinite life of enterprise is split into artificial regular time intervals. Preparation of annual financial statements, quarterly interim reporting.
Historical Cost Assets are recorded at their original acquisition price. Depreciation charged on acquisition cost; ignores inflation unless revalued under AS 10.
Dual Aspect (Duality) Every transaction has a dual effect: Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity. Basis of double-entry bookkeeping; debits always equal credits.
Matching Principle Expenses incurred to generate revenue of a period must be matched in that period. Recognition of outstanding expenses, prepaid expenses, and unearned revenue.
Prudence (Conservatism) Do not anticipate profits, but provide for all possible future losses. Valuation of inventory at lower of cost or NRV; provision for bad & doubtful debts.
Substance over Form Transactions are recorded based on their financial and economic reality, not merely legal form. Capitalization of assets acquired under finance lease / hire purchase.
Materiality Only items that meaningfully influence economic decisions of users need strict accounting. Expensing low-cost durable assets (staplers, paperweights) immediately to P&L.
Error Category Trial Balance Balances? Examples & Rectification Approach
Error of Complete Omission YES (Balances) Transaction omitted from both debit and credit. Rectification: Record the journal entry normally.
Error of Principle YES (Balances) Violates capital vs revenue rules (e.g., machinery purchased debited to Purchases). Rectification: Debit Machinery, Credit Purchases.
Compensating Error YES (Balances) Two or more independent errors cancel each other out arithmetically.
Error of Commission (Two-sided) YES (Balances) Wrong amount entered in primary subsidiary book and posted to both accounts equally.
One-Sided Errors NO (Imbalance) Undercast/overcast of subsidiary books; posting to wrong side or wrong amount in one ledger account. Rectification involves Suspense Account.
Prior Period Errors Detected after year-end Rectified in next FY via Profit & Loss Adjustment Account to protect current year's operating results.
Classification Definition & Examples Balance Sheet Translation & Gain/Loss Treatment
Monetary Items Units of currency held, receivables, payables, loans in fixed/determinable amounts. Translated at Closing Rate. Exchange differences recognized in Profit & Loss.
Non-Monetary at Historical Cost Fixed assets, inventories, investments in equity shares. Translated at Exchange Rate on the Date of Transaction (no subsequent exchange gain/loss).
Non-Monetary at Fair Value Revalued assets, investments carried at fair value. Translated at Exchange Rate on the Date Fair Value was Determined.
Non-Integral Foreign Operation (NIFO) Overseas branch/subsidiary operating with autonomy and independent cash flows. Exchange differences accumulated in Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR) in Equity.
Hedging Forward Contract Contract to hedge existing foreign currency monetary item. Premium/Discount amortized over contract life; exchange diff recognized in P&L.
Speculative Forward Contract Contract entered into for trading or speculation. No premium amortization; Marked to Market at balance sheet date with gain/loss in P&L.
Activity Definition & Inclusions Exam Special Cases
Operating Activities Principal revenue-producing activities and other non-investing/financing activities. Cash from customers, payments to suppliers and employees. Interest/dividends paid & received are Operating for financial enterprises. Direct taxes paid are Operating unless specifically identifiable.
Investing Activities Acquisition and disposal of long-term assets and other investments not included in cash equivalents. Purchase/sale of PPE, intangibles, purchase/sale of shares/debentures of other firms. Dividend and interest received by non-financial entities is Investing. Insurance claims for building/plant damage are extraordinary Investing inflows.
Financing Activities Activities that alter the size and composition of owner's equity and borrowings. Issue of shares/debentures, bank loans, redemption of preference shares, buy-back. Dividend and interest paid by non-financial entities is Financing. Bank overdrafts repayable on demand forming part of cash management are Cash Equivalents.
