Chapter III: Prospectus and Allotment of Securities (Sections 23 to 42)

SECTIONS 23 – 42

1. Public Offer vs. Private Placement (Section 23)

  • Public Company (Section 23(1)): May issue securities to public through a prospectus (IPO, FPO, Offer for Sale — OFS under Section 28), or through a rights issue/bonus issue, or through Private Placement (Section 42).
  • Private Company (Section 23(2)): May issue securities by way of rights issue, bonus issue, or private placement. Strictly prohibited from inviting the public to subscribe.

2. Types of Prospectus

Type of Prospectus Section Key Statutory Rules & Timelines
Deemed Prospectus Section 25 Any document offering allotted securities for sale to public. Presumption: offer made within 6 months of allotment OR consideration not received.
Shelf Prospectus Section 31 Issued for one or more issues without fresh prospectus. Validity: Max 1 year from date of opening of first offer. Must file Information Memorandum in Form PAS-2 before subsequent issue.
Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) Section 32 Does not include complete particulars of quantum or price. Must be filed with RoC at least 3 days prior to opening of subscription. Final prospectus filed upon closing.
Abridged Prospectus Section 33 Memorandum containing salient features specified by SEBI. Must accompany every application form (exempt: underwriting agreements, non-public offers).

3. Civil & Criminal Liabilities for Misstatements

  • Criminal Liability (Section 34): Every person authorizing issue of a prospectus containing untrue/misleading statements is liable for fraud under Section 447 (Imprisonment: 6 months to 10 years; Fine: amount involved up to 3 times).
  • Civil Liability (Section 35): Company and directors/promoters/experts personally liable to pay compensation for loss suffered by subscribers.
  • Class Action (Section 37): Affected investors can file collective suits under Sections 34, 35, or 36.

4. Allotment & Minimum Subscription (Section 39)

📋 Section 39: Minimum Subscription & Refund Deadlines
Minimum Subscription = 90% of Offer (SEBI ICDR) • Application Money ≥ 5% of Nominal Value (Sec 39(2))
• If minimum subscription is not received within 30 days of prospectus issue, all application money must be refunded within 15 days of closure.
• Default in refund: Directors are jointly and severally liable to repay with 15% per annum interest.
• Return of Allotment: Must be filed with RoC in Form PAS-3 within 30 days of allotment.

5. Private Placement (Section 42 & Rule 14)

  • Maximum Limit: Offer can be made to not more than 200 persons in aggregate in a financial year (excluding QIBs and ESOP employees). Exceeding 200 is deemed a Public Offer.
  • Mode of Payment: Must be paid through banking channels (cheque, draft, NEFT/RTGS). CASH IS STRICTLY PROHIBITED. Monies kept in separate bank account in a scheduled bank.
  • Allotment Window: Allotment must be completed within 60 days of receipt of application money. If not allotted, refund within 15 days, else pay 12% p.a. interest.
  • Penalty for Contravention (Section 42(10)): Penalty up to amount involved in offer OR Rs 2 crore, whichever is lower, plus refund with 12% p.a. interest within 30 days.

Chapter IV: Share Capital and Debentures (Sections 43 to 72)

SECTIONS 43 – 72

1. Kinds of Share Capital (Section 43)

1. Equity Share Capital: (i) with voting rights; or (ii) with differential rights as to dividend, voting or otherwise (DVRs capped at 74% of total post-issue voting power under Rule 4).
2. Preference Share Capital: Carries preferential right to fixed dividend and preferential right to repayment of capital on winding up.

2. Key Capital Provisions

Section Provision Core Statutory Mandate
Section 52 Securities Premium Account Permitted uses strictly limited to: (1) Fully paid bonus shares, (2) Writing off preliminary expenses, (3) Writing off issue expenses/commission, (4) Premium on redemption of preference shares/debentures, (5) Buy-back under Sec 68.
Section 53 Prohibition of Discount Issue of shares at discount is completely void. Exception: Sweat Equity (Sec 54) and debt restructuring conversion (Sec 53(2A)).
Section 54 Sweat Equity Shares Requires Special Resolution; Mandatory 3-year lock-in period from allotment.
Section 55 Preference Shares Irredeemable preference shares prohibited. Max tenure: 20 years (Infrastructure companies: up to 30 years with min 10% annual redemption from 21st year). Redemption out of profits creates Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR).
Section 62 Rights Issue & ESOP Rights offer open for 15–30 days (min 7 days with 90% consent); carries right of renunciation. ESOP requires Special Resolution (Ordinary for private cos).
Section 63 Bonus Shares Issued out of free reserves, securities premium, or CRR. Revaluation reserves cannot be capitalized. Cannot issue in lieu of dividend. Once announced by Board, cannot be withdrawn (Rule 14).
Section 66 Reduction of Capital Requires Special Resolution AND confirmation by NCLT. Creditors must consent or be settled.

3. Debentures (Section 71)

  • No Voting Rights: Section 71(2) strictly prohibits debentures with voting rights.
  • Debenture Redemption Reserve (DRR): Must be created out of profits available for dividend (Section 71(4)).
  • Debenture Trustee: Mandatory if offer made to more than 500 persons (Section 71(5)).

Chapter VIII: Declaration and Payment of Dividend (Sections 123 to 127)

SECTIONS 123 – 127
Figure 8.1: The Statutory Dividend Timeline & IEPF Transfer Lifecycle
Mandatory statutory sequence for dividend distribution, separate banking deposit, unpaid account, and final transfer to IEPF.
📜 Section 123: Rules for Dividend Declaration out of Reserves (Rule 3)
1. Rate ≤ Avg of Preceding 3 Years • 2. Max Withdrawal ≤ 10% of Paid-up Capital + Free Reserves • 3. Remaining Reserves ≥ 15% of Paid-up Share Capital
• Transfer to reserves prior to dividend declaration is purely voluntary / discretionary (Proviso to Sec 123(1)).
• Interim dividend declared by Board out of P&L surplus or current financial year profits.
• Non-payment Penalty (Section 127): Directors face up to 2 years imprisonment and min Rs 1,000/day fine; company must pay 18% per annum simple interest.

Chapter X: Audit and Auditors (Sections 139 to 148)

SECTIONS 139 – 148

1. Appointment & Rotation of Statutory Auditors (Section 139)

Auditor Type Appointing Authority & Timeline Tenure
First Auditor (Non-Govt) Board within 30 days of registration; if Board fails, Members at EGM within 90 days. Holds office till conclusion of 1st AGM.
First Auditor (Govt Co) CAG within 60 days of registration; if CAG fails, Board within 30 days; if Board fails, Members at EGM within 60 days. Holds office till conclusion of 1st AGM.
Subsequent Auditor (Govt Co) CAG appoints within 180 days from commencement of the financial year. Holds office till conclusion of AGM.
Casual Vacancy (Resignation) Board fills within 30 days, AND approved by Members in general meeting within 3 months of Board recommendation. Holds office till next AGM.

2. Qualifications, Disqualifications & Prohibitions (Section 141 & 144)

⚖️ Section 141: Key Disqualification Thresholds
Ceiling on Audits: Max 20 Companies (Sec 141(3)(g)) • Relative Security Holding: Max Rs 1,00,000 Face Value • Indebtedness Limit: Exceeding Rs 5,00,000 • Guarantee Limit: Exceeding Rs 1,00,000
• 20-Company Limit Exclusions: OPCs, dormant companies, small companies, and private companies with paid-up capital < Rs 100 crore without filing defaults.
• Prohibited Services (Section 144): Accounting, internal audit, financial info systems design, actuarial, investment advisory/banking, management services.
• Incurring disqualification leads to automatic vacation of office (Section 141(4)).

3. Fraud Reporting (Section 143(12)) & Cost Audit (Section 148)

  • Fraud ≥ Rs 1 Crore: Report to Board/Audit Committee within 2 days; seek reply within 45 days; forward report in Form ADT-4 to Central Government (Secretary, MCA) within 15 days of reply. Penalty for failure (Sec 143(15)): Rs 5 lakh for listed, Rs 1 lakh for unlisted.
  • Fraud < Rs 1 Crore: Report to Board/Audit Committee within 2 days; disclose in Board's Report.
  • Cost Audit (Section 148): Appointed in Form CRA-2 within 180 days of FY commencement. Submits report in Form CRA-3 to Board within 180 days of FY closure; Company submits to Central Govt in Form CRA-4 within 30 days of receipt. Statutory auditor cannot be cost auditor.

Chapter XI: Appointment and Qualifications of Directors (Sections 149 to 172)

SECTIONS 149 – 172
Figure 11.1: Board Composition, Thresholds & Rotation Mechanics
Board strength caps, gender and resident diversity mandates, independent director criteria, and rotation formulas.

1. Director Disqualifications & Vacations

  • Section 164(1) Personal Disqualifications: Unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, convicted ≥ 6 months (disqualified for 5 years; if convicted ≥ 7 years, disqualified for life), calls unpaid ≥ 6 months, no DIN.
  • Section 164(2) Company Defaults:
    • Failed to file financial statements or annual returns for continuous 3 financial years.
    • Failed to repay deposits/interest, redeem debentures, or pay declared dividend for 1 year or more.
    • Consequence: Disqualified for 5 years from appointment/reappointment in any company. Vacates office in all other companies except defaulting company (Proviso to Sec 167(1)).
  • Section 167(1)(b) Meeting Absence: Vacates office if absent from all Board meetings held during 12 months with or without leave of absence.
  • Maximum Directorships (Section 165): Max 20 companies (Max 10 public companies). Penalty: Rs 2,000/day up to Rs 2 lakh.

Chapter XII: Meetings of Board and its Powers (Sections 173 to 195)

SECTIONS 173 – 195

1. Board Meetings & Quorum Rules

  • Frequency (Section 173): Min 4 meetings/year; max gap ≤ 120 days. Notice: Min 7 days in writing (shorter notice if ≥ 1 independent director attends or ratifies).
  • Quorum (Section 174): 1/3rd of total strength or 2 directors, whichever is higher (fractions rounded to 1). Video conferencing participants counted.
  • Interested Directors: Excluded from quorum. If interested directors ≥ 2/3rds of total strength, remaining non-interested directors (min 2) form quorum.

2. Key Board Powers & Approvals (Sections 179 to 188)

Section Subject Matter Approval Required & Key Limits
Section 179(3) Powers exercisable only at Board Meetings Calls on shares, buy-back up to 10%, issuing securities/debentures, borrowing, investing, financial statements. Cannot be passed by circulation.
Section 180 Restrictions on Powers of Board Special Resolution required for: (a) Selling/disposing whole or substantially whole of undertaking; (b) Borrowing exceeding paid-up capital + free reserves + securities premium.
Section 181 Charitable Contributions Board can contribute up to 5% of average net profits of preceding 3 FYs. Prior general meeting approval required if exceeding 5%.
Section 182 Political Contributions Prohibited for Government cos and cos < 3 years old. Must be approved by Board via banking channels. Disclosure in P&L account.
Section 185 Loans to Directors Prohibited for directors/holding co directors. Permitted to entities where director is interested subject to Special Resolution and borrower using funds for principal business.
Section 186 Inter-corporate Loans & Investments Limit: 60% of Capital + Free Reserves + Securities Premium OR 100% of Free Reserves + Securities Premium, whichever is more. Special Resolution required if exceeded. Min interest rate = prevailing G-Sec yield.
Section 188 Related Party Transactions (RPT) Board approval (Audit Committee pre-approval). Ordinary Resolution required if exceeding prescribed turnover/net worth caps. Arm's length ordinary commercial transactions exempt.

Chapter XXVII: NCLT & NCLAT (Sections 407 to 434)

SECTIONS 407 – 434
Figure 27.1: NCLT and NCLAT Judicial Hierarchy & Appeal Timelines
Appellate workflow, age limits, and statutory limitation windows from NCLT to NCLAT to Supreme Court of India.

1. Qualifications & Age Limits (Sections 409 & 411)

Position / Member Qualifications Term & Age Limit
President of NCLT Judge of a High Court for at least 5 years. 5 years (re-appointable); Max age: 67 years (Min age: 50 yrs).
Judicial Member (NCLT) High Court Judge, or District Judge for ≥ 5 years, or Advocate for ≥ 10 years. 5 years; Max age: 65 years.
Technical Member (NCLT) Member of ICLS / Joint Secretary, or practicing CA, CS, or CMA for at least 15 years. 5 years; Max age: 65 years.
Chairperson (NCLAT) Judge of the Supreme Court or Chief Justice of a High Court. 5 years (re-appointable); Max age: 70 years.
Members of NCLAT Judicial: High Court Judge; Technical: Person of proven standing in law, management, finance. 5 years; Max age: 67 years.

2. Key Judicial Powers & Prohibitions

  • Appeals to NCLAT (Section 421): Filed within 45 days; extendable by max 45 days if sufficient cause. Consent orders cannot be appealed (Sec 421(2)).
  • Appeals to Supreme Court (Section 423): Filed within 60 days (extendable by 60 days) on ANY QUESTION OF LAW.
  • Expeditious Disposal (Section 422): Endeavor to dispose of petitions within 3 months; extendable by max 90 days by President/Chairperson.
  • Contempt Powers (Section 425): Vested with same contempt powers as High Court under the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971.
  • Bar of Civil Court Jurisdiction (Section 430): No civil court has jurisdiction to entertain any suit/proceeding in respect of matters NCLT/NCLAT is empowered to determine; no injunction can be granted.
  • Right to Legal Representation (Section 432): Parties can appear in person or authorize Chartered Accountants, Company Secretaries, Cost Accountants, or Legal Practitioners.
