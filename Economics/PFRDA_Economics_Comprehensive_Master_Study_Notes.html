01. Demand and Supply, Elasticity & Consumer Behaviour

MICROECONOMICS

1. The Law of Demand & Its Determinants

The Law of Demand states that, holding other factors constant (ceteris paribus), an inverse relationship exists between the price of a good and the quantity demanded: P ↑ &implies; Qd ↓.

  • Movement along the Curve: Caused strictly by a change in the own price of the good (expansion/contraction of demand).
  • Shift of the Curve: Caused by changes in non-price determinants (consumer income, tastes/preferences, substitute/complement prices, expectations).
  • Giffen Goods: Extreme inferior staple goods where the negative income effect of a price fall outweighs the positive substitution effect, producing an upward-sloping demand curve.
  • Veblen Goods: Luxury prestige items consumed conspicuously to project social status and wealth (P ↑ &implies; Qd ↑).
Figure 1.1: Market Equilibrium, Consumer Surplus & Producer Surplus
At price P* and quantity Q*, economic welfare is maximized with zero deadweight loss.
📐 Price Elasticity of Demand (Point & Percentage Method)
ep = % Δ Qd% Δ P = ΔQΔP × PQ | Geometric: ep = Lower SegmentUpper Segment
Where: ΔQ = Change in Quantity Demanded • ΔP = Change in Price • P = Initial Price • Q = Initial Quantity.
Key Thresholds: |ep| > 1: Elastic (Price ↑ &implies; Total Revenue ↓) • |ep| < 1: Inelastic (Price ↑ &implies; Total Revenue ↑) • |ep| = 1: Unitary (Revenue unchanged).
⚖️ Consumer Equilibrium & Cross/Income Elasticities
MRSxy = MUxMUy = PxPy | exy = % Δ Qx% Δ Py | ey = % Δ Q% Δ Y
Cross Elasticity (exy): exy > 0 for Substitutes (Tea/Coffee) • exy < 0 for Complements (Car/Petrol) • exy = 0 for Independent Goods.
Income Elasticity (ey): ey > 1: Luxury • 0 < ey < 1: Normal Necessity • ey < 0: Inferior Good (Engel Curve bends backward).

2. Indifference Curve Analysis

  • Indifference Curve Properties: Downward-sloping, convex to origin (Diminishing Marginal Rate of Substitution: $MRS_{xy} = MU_x / MU_y$), higher curve = higher satisfaction, curves never intersect.
  • Price Effect Decomposition: $\text{Price Effect} = \text{Substitution Effect} + \text{Income Effect}$.
    • Hicksian Method: Adjusts income to maintain the consumer on the original Indifference Curve (constant utility).
    • Slutsky Method: Adjusts income so the consumer can just purchase the original bundle at new relative prices (constant purchasing power).

02. Production, Costs & Market Structures

MICROECONOMICS

1. Production Functions & Cost Envelopes

🏭 Cobb-Douglas Production Function & Returns to Scale
Q = A · Lα · Kβ | MRTSLK = MPLMPK = wr
Where: Q = Output • A = Total Factor Productivity (TFP) • L = Labor • K = Capital • α, β = Output elasticities.
Returns to Scale: α + β = 1: Constant (CRS) • α + β > 1: Increasing (IRS) • α + β < 1: Decreasing (DRS).
Figure 2.1: Sweezy's Kinked Demand Curve & Discontinuous MR in Oligopoly
Because Marginal Cost shifts within the vertical discontinuity of MR, equilibrium price P* remains sticky.
📊 Monopoly Power & Market Concentration Metrics
Lerner Index (L) = P − MCP = 1|ε| | HHI = ∑ (si)2
Lerner Index: Measures pricing mark-up over Marginal Cost ($P − MC$). Under Perfect Competition, $P = MC \implies L = 0$. Pure Monopoly: $L \to 1$.
HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index): Sum of squared percentage market shares. Monopolies score 10,000. Moderate concentration: 1,500–2,500; High concentration: > 2,500.

2. Comparative Market Structures

Market Type Firms & Product Demand Curve & Pricing Long-Run Equilibrium & Efficiency
Perfect Competition Infinite sellers; Homogeneous product. Horizontal demand ($P = AR = MR$). Price taker. $P = MR = LMC = \text{Min } LAC$. Zero economic profit. Productive & Allocative efficiency.
Pure Monopoly Single seller; Unique product with no close substitutes. Downward-sloping demand ($P > MR$). Price maker. $MR = MC$; Persistent supernormal profits. Causes Deadweight Loss. Lerner Index: $L = (P-MC)/P = 1/|\epsilon|$.
Monopolistic Competition Many sellers; Differentiated products. Downward-sloping, highly elastic demand. Tangency of demand with downward-sloping LAC. Zero economic profit, but suffers Excess Capacity.
Oligopoly Few dominant interdependent sellers. Strategic interaction / Kinked demand curve. Models: Cournot (quantity), Bertrand (price wars: $P=MC$), Stackelberg (leader-follower), Sweezy (price stickiness).

03. National Income Accounting & Circular Flow

MACROECONOMICS
💰 Core National Income Accounting & Deflator Identities
GDP Deflator = Nominal GDPReal GDP × 100 | Y = C + I + G + (X − M)
Gross National Product (GNP): GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA).
Net National Product at Factor Cost (NNPFC / National Income): NNPFC = NNPMP − Net Indirect Taxes (Indirect Taxes − Subsidies).
India MoSPI System: GVA at Basic Prices = GVAFC + (Production Taxes − Production Subsidies); GDPMP = GVABasic + (Product Taxes − Product Subsidies).
Circular Flow Equilibrium: Injections (I + G + X) = Leakages (S + T + M).

Measurement Approaches & Inclusions/Exclusions

  • Production (Value Added) Method: $\text{GVA} = \text{Gross Value of Output} - \text{Intermediate Consumption}$. Prevents double-counting.
  • Income Method: $\text{NDP}_{FC} = \text{Compensation of Employees} + \text{Operating Surplus (Rent, Interest, Profit)} + \text{Mixed Income of Self-Employed}$.
  • Expenditure Method: $Y = C + I + G + (X - M)$, where $C$ is Private Consumption, $I$ is Gross Capital Formation, $G$ is Government Final Spending, and $(X - M)$ is Net Exports.
  • Exclusions from GDP: Second-hand asset sales (already counted in original year), transfer payments (pensions, doles, scholarships) because no productive output is generated in exchange.
  • Green GDP: Deducts the monetary cost of natural resource depletion and environmental degradation: $\text{Green GDP} = \text{GDP} - \text{Environmental Damage Costs}$.

04. Classical vs. Keynesian Approaches

MACROECONOMIC THEORIES
Feature Classical Economic Model Keynesian Macroeconomic Model
Governing Law Say's Law: "Supply creates its own demand". Total factor payments equal total output. Principle of Effective Demand: Demand creates its own supply. $AD$ determines output.
Wage-Price Mechanism Perfectly flexible wages and prices clear markets instantaneously. Wages and prices are 'sticky downwards' due to contracts, minimum wage, and unions.
Employment Full employment is automatic. Unemployment is strictly voluntary or frictional. Underemployment equilibrium is persistent. Involuntary unemployment is normal in slumps.
Money Neutrality Classical Dichotomy: Money is neutral ($MV=PT$), affecting only nominal prices. Money affects real output and employment via interest rates and liquidity preference.
Saving-Investment Equilibrated by flexible real interest rates in loanable funds markets. Equilibrated by changes in aggregate national income ($Y$).
Fiscal Stance Laissez-faire; balanced budgets. State intervention causes crowding out. Active counter-cyclical fiscal intervention (deficit spending) needed to boost demand.
Keynes' Three Motives for Holding Money:
1. Transactions Motive ($L_1(Y)$): Money held for day-to-day expenditure; positive function of income.
2. Precautionary Motive ($L_1(Y)$): Money held for unexpected contingencies/emergencies; function of income.
3. Speculative Motive ($L_2(r)$): Money held to exploit future changes in bond prices; inversely related to interest rate $r$.

05. Consumption & Investment Functions

MACROECONOMIC AGGREGATES
📈 Consumption Functions & Tobin's Q Investment Ratio
C = C̄ + c · Y | MPC + MPS = 1 | Tobin's q = Market Value of Capital StockReplacement Cost of Physical Capital
Where: C̄ = Autonomous Consumption • c = Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC = ΔC/ΔY, 0 < c < 1) • MPS = Marginal Propensity to Save (ΔS/ΔY).
Friedman's PIH: C = k · Yp (Driven by Permanent Income; transitory MPC ≈ 0) • Modigliani's LCH: C = αW + βY (Consumption smoothed over lifespan).
Tobin's q Decision: q > 1 &implies; Invest in physical capital • q < 1 &implies; Acquire existing firms / Hold investment.

Theories of the Investment Function

  • Marginal Efficiency of Capital (MEC): Discount rate equating expected future yields of an asset to its supply price (replacement cost). Invest if $\text{MEC} > r$.
  • Marginal Efficiency of Investment (MEI): Takes into account rising capital goods prices during aggregate investment booms; MEI is steeper than MEC.

06. Multiplier, Accelerator & Super-Multiplier

MACROECONOMIC DYNAMICS
⚡ Multiplier Formulas & The Accelerator Principle
Investment Multiplier (k) = 11 − MPC = 1MPS | Tax Multiplier (kt) = −MPCMPS
Balanced Budget (kbb) = 1 (ΔY = ΔG) | Foreign Trade (kf) = 1MPS + MPM | Accelerator: It = v · (Yt − Yt-1)
Super-Multiplier: Combines multiplier and accelerator: initial autonomous spending boosts income → induces capital investment via accelerator → feeds back into multiplier.
Numerical Example: If MPC = 0.80 &implies; k = 1 / (1 − 0.80) = 1 / 0.20 = 5. A Rs 1,000 Cr investment expands national income by Rs 5,000 Cr.
⚡ Paradox of Thrift (Keynes): If every household attempts to increase its saving rate during a recession, aggregate consumption collapses. Because one person's spending is another's income, national income plunges until total savings drops back to equal the unchanged investment level.

07. The IS-LM Framework & Macroeconomic Policy

HICKS-HANSEN SYNTHESIS
🔄 The IS-LM Equilibrium Equations
IS Curve: Y = C(Y − T) + I(r) + G | LM Curve: MP = L1(Y) + L2(r)
IS Curve (Goods Market): Downward sloping (r↑ → I↓ → Y↓). Shifts right by ΔG × k with expansionary fiscal policy.
LM Curve (Money Market): Upward sloping (Y↑ → L1↑ → r↑). Shifts right by ΔM/P with expansionary monetary policy.
Crowding-Out Effect: Fiscal expansion shifts IS right, driving up interest rates along LM, dampening interest-sensitive private capital investment.
Figure 7.1: The IS-LM Equilibrium & Fiscal vs. Monetary Policy Shifts
Fiscal expansion (IS right) raises Y and r (crowding out). Monetary expansion (LM right) raises Y and lowers r.
Special Case Curve Shape Fiscal Policy Power Monetary Policy Power
Liquidity Trap LM curve is perfectly horizontal ($r \approx 0$). 100% Maximum Power: Full multiplier effect; zero crowding out. 0% Impotent: Money injections hoarded as cash ('pushing on string').
Classical Case LM curve is perfectly vertical ($h = 0$). 0% Impotent: 100% complete crowding-out of private investment. 100% Maximum Power: Directly expands output.

08. Inflation Dynamics & The Phillips Curve

MACROECONOMIC STABILIZATION
📉 Expectations-Augmented Phillips Curve (Friedman-Phelps)
π = πe − β(u − u*) + v | Sacrifice Ratio = % Cumulative Real GDP LossPercentage Point Drop in π
Where: π = Actual Inflation Rate • πe = Expected Inflation • u = Actual Unemployment Rate • u* = Natural Rate of Unemployment (NAIRU) • β = Response coefficient • v = Supply Shock.
Long-Run Equilibrium: In the long run, π = πe &implies; u = u* (LRPC is perfectly vertical at NAIRU; no permanent trade-off exists).

09. Business Cycles & Economic Fluctuations

MACROECONOMIC CYCLES

1. The Four Phases of a Business Cycle

1. Expansion (Boom): Rising GDP, expanding employment, rising investment, optimistic business confidence.
2. Peak: Capacity constraints reached, factor shortages emerge, inflation accelerates.
3. Contraction (Recession): Falling GDP, declining profits, rising unemployment. Technical recession = $\ge 2$ consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth.
4. Trough (Recovery): Bottom of slump; low input prices and replacement investment initiate recovery.

2. Major Business Cycle Theories

  • Schumpeter's Innovation Theory: Waves of entrepreneurial innovation drive cycles through Creative Destruction.
  • Keynesian Multiplier-Accelerator Interaction: Volatility in autonomous investment driven by business confidence ('animal spirits') amplified by super-multiplier swings.
  • Monetarist Theory (Milton Friedman): Unsteady, erratic central bank money supply growth causes booms and busts.
  • Real Business Cycle (RBC) Theory (Kydland & Prescott): Cycles are optimal market equilibrium responses to real technology and productivity supply shocks.
  • Austrian Theory (Hayek & Mises): Artificial central bank interest rate suppression generates credit-fueled malinvestment that eventually crashes.
  • Inverted Yield Curve: When short-term bond yields exceed long-term bond yields (e.g. 2-yr yield > 10-yr yield), historically signaling impending recession within 12-18 months.

10. Balance of Payments (BOP) Architecture

EXTERNAL SECTOR
BOP Account Sub-Components & Inclusions
Current Account • Merchandise Trade: Visible exports & imports.
• Services (Invisibles): IT software exports, tourism, transport, financial services.
• Primary Income: Investment income (dividends, interest, profits) & compensation of employees.
• Secondary Income: Unrequited transfers (diaspora remittances, gifts, foreign grants).
Capital Account (BPM6) Capital transfers (migrant transfers, debt forgiveness) & acquisition/disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets (patents, trademarks, mineral rights).
Financial Account • FDI (Foreign Direct Investment): $\ge 10\%$ equity stake with lasting management control.
• FPI (Foreign Portfolio Investment): Passive liquid shares, bonds ('hot money').
• ECB: External Commercial Borrowings raised by Indian corporates.
• NRI Deposits: FCNR(B), NRE, NRO accounts.
• Reserve Assets: Changes in foreign exchange reserves held by the RBI.
Autonomous vs. Accommodating Transactions:
• Autonomous ('Above the line'): Commercial transactions undertaken for independent profit motives.
• Accommodating ('Below the line'): Financing items undertaken by the central bank (drawing down forex reserves) to bridge the autonomous deficit.

11. Foreign Exchange (Forex) & Parities

INTERNATIONAL FINANCE
🌐 Foreign Exchange Parities & The Marshall-Lerner Condition
Relative PPP: % ΔS ≈ πdomestic − πforeign | CIP: F − SS ≈ i − i*
REER = NEER × PdomesticPforeign | Marshall-Lerner: |ex + em| > 1
Purchasing Power Parity (PPP): Absolute: S = P / P*. Relative: Currency depreciates by inflation differential.
Covered Interest Parity (CIP): Forward premium (F − S)/S eliminates riskless international interest arbitrage.
Marshall-Lerner Condition: Currency devaluation/depreciation improves the trade balance if and only if the sum of export and import price elasticities exceeds 1 (|ex + em| > 1). In the short run, $|e_x + e_m| < 1$, causing the initial J-Curve deterioration.

Because trade contracts are pre-fixed, the trade deficit initially worsens before improving as export/import volumes adjust.

12. Monetary Policy, Fiscal Policy & NBFCs

REGULATORY & FINANCIAL ARCHITECTURE
🏛️ Public Finance Deficit Identities (Union Budget Metrics)
Fiscal Deficit (FD) = Total Expenditure − (Revenue Receipts + Non-Debt Capital Receipts)
Revenue Deficit (RD) = Revenue Exp − Revenue Rec | Effective RD = RD − Grants for Capital Assets
Primary Deficit (PD) = Fiscal Deficit − Net Interest Payments
Significance: Fiscal Deficit measures the government's total borrowing requirement from all sources.
N.K. Singh FRBM Targets: General Government Debt-to-GDP ceiling of 60% (40% Centre, 20% States) • Central Fiscal Deficit target of 3.0% of GDP • Escape Clause allowing up to 0.5% GDP deviation for national security, war, acts of God, or severe structural shocks.
🏦 Monetary Policy Corridor & NBFC Prudential Ratios
MSF Rate (Ceiling) = Repo + 0.25% | SDF Rate (Floor) = Repo − 0.25% | LAF Width = 50 bps
NBFC 50-50 Rule: Financial Assets > 50% AND Financial Income > 50% | NBFC Min CRAR = 15% (Tier-1 ≥ 10%)
Standing Deposit Facility (SDF): Uncollateralized overnight liquidity absorption facility introduced in April 2022.
Scale-Based Regulation (SBR): Base Layer (Non-deposit < Rs 1,000 Cr; P2P; AA) • Middle Layer (Deposit-taking; Assets ≥ Rs 1,000 Cr; HFCs; CICs; IFCs) • Upper Layer (Top 10 NBFCs; CET1 = 9%; Mandatory listing in 3 yrs) • Top Layer (Bespoke risk surcharge).
